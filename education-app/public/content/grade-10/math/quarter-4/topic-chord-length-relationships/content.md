## What You Should Already Know

Before starting, check that these ideas feel familiar:

- A chord is a segment whose endpoints lie on a circle.
- When two chords cross, each chord is split into two smaller segments.
- Products such as \(6 \cdot 8\) mean multiplication.
- To solve \(4x = 36\), divide both sides by 4.

### Pre-check / Recall

Try these first. Then reveal the answers.

1. What is \(7 \cdot 5\)?
2. Solve \(3x = 24\).
3. If a chord is split into lengths 4 and 9, what product can you form?
4. True or false: segment lengths in a circle diagram should be positive.

<details>
<summary>Reveal pre-check answers</summary>

1. \(35\)
2. \(x = 8\)
3. \(4 \cdot 9 = 36\)
4. True

If these felt slow, keep a multiplication table or calculator nearby while you focus on the geometry.
</details>

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chord | A segment with both endpoints on the circle |
| Intersecting chords | Two chords that cross inside the circle |
| Segment of a chord | One of the two pieces made when a chord is cut by the intersection point |
| Product | The result of multiplying two values |
| Intersecting chords theorem | The products of the two segment lengths on each chord are equal |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> If two chords intersect inside a circle, then the product of the two segments on one chord equals the product of the two segments on the other chord.

## The Formula

Suppose chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) intersect at point \(P\).

![Two chords intersecting inside a circle with products labeled](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-chord-length-relationships/images/intersecting-chords-product.svg)

The intersecting chords theorem says:

$$
AP \cdot PB = CP \cdot PD
$$

Read it as:

- multiply the two pieces of chord \(AB\)
- multiply the two pieces of chord \(CD\)
- set the products equal

> [!TIP] Pair Pieces on the Same Chord
>
> Do not multiply adjacent-looking pieces just because they are near each other. The pair must come from the same straight chord.

## Worked Example 1: Find a Missing Segment

**Problem:** Two chords intersect at \(P\). On one chord, \(AP = 6\) and \(PB = 8\). On the other chord, \(CP = 4\) and \(PD = x\). Find \(x\).

![Worked missing segment example for intersecting chords](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-chord-length-relationships/images/missing-segment-example.svg)

**Step 1: Write the theorem.**

$$
AP \cdot PB = CP \cdot PD
$$

**Step 2: Substitute the segment lengths.**

$$
6 \cdot 8 = 4 \cdot x
$$

**Step 3: Multiply and solve.**

$$
48 = 4x
$$

$$
x = 12
$$

**Answer:** \(x = 12\)

## Worked Example 2: When the Unknown Is Paired with a Number

**Problem:** Chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) intersect at \(P\). If \(AP = 5\), \(PB = x\), \(CP = 3\), and \(PD = 10\), find \(x\).

Set up the products:

$$
5x = 3 \cdot 10
$$

$$
5x = 30
$$

$$
x = 6
$$

**Answer:** \(x = 6\)

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> A chord's total length is not usually part of the theorem. Use the two segment lengths made by the intersection point. If a problem gives a total chord length, subtract first to find the missing piece.

## Strategy Checklist

![Checklist for setting up an intersecting chord equation](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-chord-length-relationships/images/setup-checklist.svg)

Use this every time:

1. Mark the intersection point.
2. Identify the two pieces of the first chord.
3. Identify the two pieces of the second chord.
4. Write \(piece \cdot piece = piece \cdot piece\).
5. Solve for the missing length.
6. Check that the answer is positive and reasonable.

## Guided Practice

### Guided Problem 1

Two chords intersect at \(P\). One chord has segments 9 and 4. The other has segments 6 and \(x\). Find \(x\).

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Multiply the two pieces on each chord.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Set \(9 \cdot 4 = 6x\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\(9 \cdot 4 = 36\), so \(36 = 6x\). Divide by 6 to get \(x = 6\).
</details>

### Guided Problem 2

Two chords intersect at \(P\). On chord \(AB\), \(AP = 7\) and \(PB = 12\). On chord \(CD\), \(CP = x\) and \(PD = 6\). Find \(x\).

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use \(AP \cdot PB = CP \cdot PD\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Substitute: \(7 \cdot 12 = x \cdot 6\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\(84 = 6x\), so \(x = 14\).
</details>

### Guided Problem 3

A problem gives one chord with total length 15. One segment of that chord is 6, so the other segment is 9. The other chord has segments 3 and \(x\). Find \(x\).

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Use the two pieces of the first chord, not the total length 15.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Set \(6 \cdot 9 = 3x\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\(54 = 3x\), so \(x = 18\).
</details>

## Mini-Quiz

Try these without looking back.

1. Fill in the formula: \(AP \cdot PB = \_\_\_\_\).
2. Chord segments are 8 and 5 on one chord, and 4 and \(x\) on the other. What is \(x\)?
3. True or false: You may multiply one segment from each different chord as the first product.
4. A chord has total length 20 and one segment is 7. What is the other segment?

<details>
<summary>Reveal mini-quiz answers</summary>

1. \(CP \cdot PD\)
2. \(8 \cdot 5 = 4x\), so \(x = 10\)
3. False. Pair the two pieces on the same chord.
4. \(13\)
</details>

## Final Checklist

You are ready for practice when you can:

- identify the two pieces on each chord
- write the product equation without mixing chords
- solve one-step equations from the chord products
- subtract from a total chord length when a segment is not given directly
- reject negative segment lengths as unreasonable
