## Warm-Up Recall

Before using exterior angle rules, check these circle facts.

1. A secant intersects a circle at how many points?
2. A tangent touches a circle at how many points?
3. If an angle intercepts a \(120^\circ\) arc and a \(40^\circ\) arc, what is the difference of the arcs?
4. What is half of \(80^\circ\)?

<details>
<summary>Reveal warm-up answers</summary>

1. Two points.
2. One point.
3. \(120^\circ - 40^\circ = 80^\circ\).
4. \(40^\circ\).
</details>

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exterior angle | An angle with its vertex outside the circle |
| Secant | A line that intersects a circle at two points |
| Tangent | A line that touches a circle at exactly one point |
| Intercepted arcs | The arcs cut off by the two lines forming the angle |
| Far arc | The larger or farther intercepted arc |
| Near arc | The smaller or nearer intercepted arc |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Rule
>
> For an angle formed outside a circle by two secants, two tangents, or a tangent and a secant:
>
> \[
> m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(\text{larger intercepted arc} - \text{smaller intercepted arc})
> \]

## Why the Rule Uses a Difference

Exterior circle angles are smaller than the big arc they reach across. The near arc "subtracts" part of that spread.

So the thinking pattern is:

1. Identify the two intercepted arcs.
2. Subtract smaller from larger.
3. Take half.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> Do not add the arcs for exterior angles. Adding is a common mistake from interior-angle problems. Exterior secant and tangent angle problems use half the **difference**.

## Case 1: Secant-Secant

Two secants start from the same exterior point and cut through the circle.

![Two secants forming an exterior angle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-external-secant-and-tangent-angles/images/secant-secant-exterior-angle.svg)

### Worked Example

**Problem:** Two secants form an exterior angle. The intercepted arcs measure \(150^\circ\) and \(50^\circ\). Find the angle.

**Solution:**

\[
m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(150^\circ - 50^\circ)
\]

\[
m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(100^\circ) = 50^\circ
\]

**Answer:** \(50^\circ\)

> [!CHECK] Quick Check
>
> If the arcs are \(132^\circ\) and \(48^\circ\), the exterior angle is \(\frac{1}{2}(132 - 48) = 42^\circ\).

## Case 2: Tangent-Secant

A tangent touches the circle once. A secant crosses the circle twice. If they meet outside the circle, the same half-difference rule applies.

![A tangent and a secant forming an exterior angle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-external-secant-and-tangent-angles/images/tangent-secant-exterior-angle.svg)

### Worked Example

**Problem:** A tangent and a secant form an exterior angle. The intercepted arcs are \(210^\circ\) and \(70^\circ\). Find the angle.

**Solution:**

\[
m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(210^\circ - 70^\circ)
\]

\[
m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(140^\circ) = 70^\circ
\]

**Answer:** \(70^\circ\)

## Case 3: Tangent-Tangent

Two tangents from the same exterior point touch the circle at two points. The intercepted arcs are the major arc and the minor arc between the tangent points.

![Two tangents forming an exterior angle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-external-secant-and-tangent-angles/images/tangent-tangent-exterior-angle.svg)

### Worked Example

**Problem:** Two tangents form an exterior angle. The minor arc between the tangent points is \(100^\circ\). Find the exterior angle.

**Solution:**

The major arc is:

\[
360^\circ - 100^\circ = 260^\circ
\]

Use half the difference:

\[
m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(260^\circ - 100^\circ) = 80^\circ
\]

**Answer:** \(80^\circ\)

> [!TIP] Tangent-Tangent Shortcut
>
> For two tangents, the exterior angle also equals \(180^\circ -\) the minor arc.
>
> In the example: \(180^\circ - 100^\circ = 80^\circ\).

## Solving for a Missing Arc

Sometimes the angle is given and an arc is missing. Start with the same formula, then solve.

### Example: Find the Far Arc

**Problem:** An exterior angle is \(35^\circ\). The smaller intercepted arc is \(80^\circ\). Find the larger intercepted arc \(x\).

\[
35 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 80)
\]

Double both sides:

\[
70 = x - 80
\]

Add 80:

\[
x = 150
\]

**Answer:** The larger arc is \(150^\circ\).

## Mini Drill

Try these before taking the practice exam.

1. Arcs: \(160^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\). Find the exterior angle.
2. Arcs: \(225^\circ\) and \(95^\circ\). Find the tangent-secant angle.
3. Two tangents have minor arc \(72^\circ\). Find the exterior angle.
4. An exterior angle is \(45^\circ\), and the smaller arc is \(60^\circ\). Find the larger arc.

<details>
<summary>Reveal mini drill answers</summary>

1. \(\frac{1}{2}(160 - 60) = 50^\circ\)
2. \(\frac{1}{2}(225 - 95) = 65^\circ\)
3. \(180 - 72 = 108^\circ\)
4. \(45 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 60)\), so \(90 = x - 60\), and \(x = 150^\circ\)
</details>

## Final Checklist

Before the assessment, make sure you can:

- Tell whether the figure is secant-secant, tangent-secant, or tangent-tangent.
- Find the two intercepted arcs.
- Use half the difference of the arcs.
- Use \(360^\circ\) to find the major arc in tangent-tangent problems.
- Work backward when an arc is unknown.
