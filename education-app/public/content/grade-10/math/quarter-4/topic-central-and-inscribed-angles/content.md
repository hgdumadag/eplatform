## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Central angle | An angle whose vertex is at the center of the circle |
| Inscribed angle | An angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords |
| Intercepted arc | The arc cut off by the sides of an angle |
| Minor arc | An arc measuring less than \(180^\circ\) |
| Semicircle | An arc measuring \(180^\circ\) |
| Major arc | An arc measuring more than \(180^\circ\) |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> A central angle has the same measure as its intercepted arc. An inscribed angle has half the measure of its intercepted arc.

## 1. Central Angles Match Their Intercepted Arcs

A central angle starts at the center of the circle. Its sides are radii.

![Central angle and intercepted arc](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-central-and-inscribed-angles/images/central-angle-arc.svg)

If \(\angle AOB\) is a central angle, then:

$$
m\angle AOB = m\overset{\frown}{AB}
$$

### Example 1: Central Angle to Arc

**Problem:** A central angle measures \(72^\circ\). What is the measure of its intercepted arc?

**Solution:** For a central angle, the angle and intercepted arc are equal.

$$
m\overset{\frown}{AB} = 72^\circ
$$

**Answer:** \(72^\circ\)

> [!CHECK] Quick Check
>
> If central angle \(\angle COD\) measures \(135^\circ\), then intercepted arc \(\overset{\frown}{CD}\) also measures \(135^\circ\).

## 2. Inscribed Angles Are Half Their Intercepted Arcs

An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle. Its sides are chords.

![Inscribed angle and intercepted arc](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-central-and-inscribed-angles/images/inscribed-angle-arc.svg)

If \(\angle ACB\) is an inscribed angle, then:

$$
m\angle ACB = \frac{1}{2}m\overset{\frown}{AB}
$$

You can also reverse the rule:

$$
m\overset{\frown}{AB} = 2m\angle ACB
$$

### Example 2: Arc to Inscribed Angle

**Problem:** An inscribed angle intercepts an arc measuring \(110^\circ\). What is the angle measure?

**Solution:** An inscribed angle is half its intercepted arc.

$$
m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(110^\circ) = 55^\circ
$$

**Answer:** \(55^\circ\)

### Example 3: Inscribed Angle to Arc

**Problem:** An inscribed angle measures \(38^\circ\). What is the measure of its intercepted arc?

**Solution:** The intercepted arc is twice the inscribed angle.

$$
m\overset{\frown}{arc} = 2(38^\circ) = 76^\circ
$$

**Answer:** \(76^\circ\)

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> Do not make every angle equal to its arc. That rule is only for central angles. For inscribed angles, multiply or divide by 2.

## 3. Same Arc, Related Angles

When a central angle and an inscribed angle intercept the same arc, the central angle is twice the inscribed angle.

![Central and inscribed angles intercepting the same arc](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-central-and-inscribed-angles/images/same-arc-comparison.svg)

| If they intercept the same arc | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Central angle and arc | central angle = arc |
| Inscribed angle and arc | inscribed angle = half the arc |
| Central angle and inscribed angle | central angle = twice the inscribed angle |

### Example 4: Same Intercepted Arc

**Problem:** A central angle and an inscribed angle intercept the same arc. The inscribed angle measures \(47^\circ\). What is the central angle?

**Solution:** The central angle is twice the inscribed angle.

$$
2(47^\circ) = 94^\circ
$$

**Answer:** \(94^\circ\)

Central angle \(\angle MON\) measures \(128^\circ\). What is \(m\overset{\frown}{MN}\)?

A central angle and its intercepted arc have equal measures.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

### Guided Problem 2

Inscribed angle \(\angle RST\) intercepts arc \(\overset{\frown}{RT}\), and \(m\overset{\frown}{RT} = 146^\circ\). What is \(m\angle RST\)?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

An inscribed angle is half its intercepted arc.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\(m\angle RST = \frac{1}{2}(146^\circ) = 73^\circ\)
</details>

### Guided Problem 3

An inscribed angle measures \(29^\circ\). What is the measure of its intercepted arc?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\(m\overset{\frown}{arc} = 2(29^\circ) = 58^\circ\)
</details>

## Strategy: Name the Angle Type First

Before calculating, ask:

1. Where is the vertex?
2. Is the vertex at the center or on the circle?
3. What arc is being intercepted?
4. Should I use equal, half, or double?

| Vertex location | Angle type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Center of the circle | Central angle | angle = intercepted arc |
| On the circle | Inscribed angle | angle = half intercepted arc |

> [!TIP] Fast Decision
>
> Center means same. Circle edge means half.

## Final Checklist

You are ready for practice when you can:

- identify whether an angle is central or inscribed;
- name the intercepted arc from the angle's endpoints;
- find an arc from a central angle;
- find an inscribed angle from an arc;
- double an inscribed angle to find its intercepted arc;
- explain why a central angle is twice an inscribed angle when they intercept the same arc.
