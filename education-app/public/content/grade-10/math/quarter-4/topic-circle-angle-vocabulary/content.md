## Target Vocabulary

> [!TARGET] Lesson 1 Target
>
> Define these circle terms accurately: **central angle**, **arc**, **chord**, **secant**, and **tangent**.

| Term | Definition | Fast recognition clue |
|---|---|---|
| Central angle | An angle whose vertex is at the center of a circle | The angle opens from the center |
| Arc | A connected part of the circle's curved edge | It is curved, not straight |
| Chord | A segment whose endpoints are on the circle | Both endpoints touch the circle |
| Diameter | A chord that passes through the center | Longest chord in a circle |
| Secant | A line that intersects a circle at two points | It cuts through the circle |
| Tangent | A line that touches a circle at exactly one point | It just touches once |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> Circle vocabulary is mostly about **where points are located**. Ask: Is the point at the center, on the circle, inside the circle, or outside the circle?

## 1. Central Angles and Arcs

A **central angle** has its vertex at the center of the circle. Its sides are usually radii.

In the diagram below, \(\angle AOB\) is a central angle because point \(O\) is the center.

![Central angle and arcs in a circle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-circle-angle-vocabulary/images/central-angle-and-arcs.svg)

The curved part from \(A\) to \(B\) is an **arc**. Arcs are named using points on the circle.

| Arc type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Minor arc | The shorter arc between two points on a circle |
| Major arc | The longer arc between two points on a circle |
| Semicircle | An arc that is exactly half the circle |

### Example 1: Name the Angle and Arc

**Problem:** In a circle with center \(O\), points \(A\) and \(B\) lie on the circle. Segments \(\overline{OA}\) and \(\overline{OB}\) form an angle. What kind of angle is \(\angle AOB\)?

**Solution:** The vertex \(O\) is the center of the circle, so \(\angle AOB\) is a central angle.

**Answer:** central angle

> [!TIP] Naming Reminder
>
> The center letter must be the middle letter when naming a central angle. So \(\angle AOB\) has vertex \(O\).

## 2. Chords and Diameters

A **chord** is a segment whose endpoints are both on the circle.

A **diameter** is a special chord that passes through the center of the circle.

![Chord and diameter in a circle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-circle-angle-vocabulary/images/chord-and-diameter.svg)

| Segment | What it shows |
|---|---|
| \(\overline{CD}\) | A chord because both endpoints are on the circle |
| \(\overline{EF}\) | A diameter because it is a chord through center \(O\) |

### Example 2: Is It a Chord?

**Problem:** Segment \(\overline{PQ}\) has endpoints \(P\) and \(Q\) on a circle. It does not pass through the center. Is it a chord?

**Solution:** Yes. A chord only needs both endpoints on the circle. It does not have to pass through the center.

**Answer:** \(\overline{PQ}\) is a chord, but not a diameter.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> Every diameter is a chord, but not every chord is a diameter.

## 3. Secants and Tangents

A **secant** is a line that intersects a circle at two points.

A **tangent** is a line that touches a circle at exactly one point. That one point is called the **point of tangency**.

![Secant and tangent compared outside a circle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-circle-angle-vocabulary/images/secant-vs-tangent.svg)

| Line | Number of intersection points | Vocabulary word |
|---|---:|---|
| Line through \(R\) and \(S\) | 2 | Secant |
| Line touching at \(T\) | 1 | Tangent |

### Example 3: Count the Contact Points

**Problem:** A line crosses a circle at points \(M\) and \(N\). Is the line a secant or a tangent?

**Solution:** The line meets the circle at two points. A line with two intersection points is a secant.

**Answer:** secant

> [!CHECK] Quick Check
>
> If a line touches a circle at exactly one point, what is it called?
>
> <details>
> <summary>Reveal answer</summary>
>
> It is a tangent.
>
> </details>

## Vocabulary Summary

![Circle vocabulary summary map](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-circle-angle-vocabulary/images/vocabulary-summary-map.svg)

Use these questions when you identify a diagram:

1. Is the vertex at the center? If yes, it may be a central angle.
2. Is the object a curved part of the circle? If yes, it is an arc.
3. Is it a segment with both endpoints on the circle? If yes, it is a chord.
4. Does a line cut the circle in two places? If yes, it is a secant.
5. Does a line touch the circle in one place? If yes, it is a tangent.
