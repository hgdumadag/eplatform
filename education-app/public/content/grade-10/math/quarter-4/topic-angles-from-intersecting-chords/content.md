## What You Should Already Know

Before using the formula, make sure these ideas feel familiar:

- A chord is a segment with endpoints on a circle.
- An arc is part of the circle between two points.
- Vertical angles are opposite angles formed by two intersecting lines, and they are congruent.
- If an angle is half of a sum, then the sum is twice the angle.

### Pre-check / Readiness Quiz

1. What is half of \(140^\circ\)?
2. If \(x + 80 = 150\), what is \(x\)?
3. True or false: Vertical angles have equal measures.
4. What does it mean for an angle to be inside a circle?

3. True.
4. Its vertex is inside the circle, not on the circle and not outside it.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chord | A segment whose endpoints are on a circle |
| Intersecting chords | Two chords that cross each other inside a circle |
| Interior angle | An angle whose vertex is inside the circle |
| Intercepted arcs | The two arcs cut off by the sides of an angle and its vertical angle |
| Minor arc | The shorter arc between two points on a circle |
| Major arc | The longer arc between two points on a circle |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> When two chords intersect inside a circle, the angle measure is half the sum of the measures of the two intercepted arcs.

## The Rule

If chords \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{CD}\) intersect at point \(P\) inside a circle, then:

$$
m\angle APC = \frac{1}{2}\left(m\widehat{AC} + m\widehat{BD}\right)
$$

The angle uses two arcs: the arc between the endpoints of the angle, and the opposite arc between the endpoints of its vertical angle.

![Two chords intersecting inside a circle with the angle rule shown](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-angles-from-intersecting-chords/images/intersecting-chords-angle-rule.svg)

> [!TIP] Read Across the Angle
>
> Do not add all four arcs. For one interior angle, use the arc opened by the angle and the arc opened by its vertical angle.

## Worked Example 1: Find an Interior Angle

**Problem:** Chords \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{CD}\) intersect inside a circle. If \(m\widehat{AC} = 80^\circ\) and \(m\widehat{BD} = 120^\circ\), find \(m\angle APC\).

**Solution:**

Use the intersecting chords formula.

$$
m\angle APC = \frac{1}{2}(80^\circ + 120^\circ)
$$

$$
m\angle APC = \frac{1}{2}(200^\circ) = 100^\circ
$$

**Answer:** \(m\angle APC = 100^\circ\)

## Worked Example 2: Find a Missing Arc

**Problem:** Two chords intersect inside a circle. An interior angle measures \(65^\circ\). One intercepted arc measures \(50^\circ\). Find the other intercepted arc.

**Solution:**

Let the missing arc be \(x\).

$$
65 = \frac{1}{2}(50 + x)
$$

Double both sides.

$$
130 = 50 + x
$$

Subtract 50.

$$
x = 80
$$

**Answer:** The missing intercepted arc is \(80^\circ\).

## Vertical Angles and Arc Pairs

Intersecting chords form two pairs of vertical angles. Each pair uses a different pair of opposite arcs.

![Vertical angles formed by intersecting chords and their opposite arc pairs](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-angles-from-intersecting-chords/images/vertical-angles-arc-pairs.svg)

If one angle uses arcs \(AC\) and \(BD\), then its vertical angle also uses arcs \(AC\) and \(BD\). The adjacent angle uses the other pair of arcs.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> The angle is not half the difference of the arcs. Half the difference is used for some exterior circle angles. Intersecting chords inside a circle use half the sum.

## Guided Practice with Revealable Hints

### Guided Problem 1

Two chords intersect inside a circle. The intercepted arcs for an angle are \(90^\circ\) and \(70^\circ\). Find the angle.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Add the two intercepted arcs.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Then take half of the sum.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\[
\frac{1}{2}(90^\circ + 70^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}(160^\circ) = 80^\circ
\]

The angle is \(80^\circ\).
</details>

### Guided Problem 2

An interior angle measures \(72^\circ\). One intercepted arc measures \(64^\circ\). Find the other intercepted arc.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The sum of the intercepted arcs is twice the angle.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Twice \(72^\circ\) is \(144^\circ\). Subtract \(64^\circ\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Let the missing arc be \(x\).

\[
72 = \frac{1}{2}(64 + x)
\]

\[
144 = 64 + x
\]

\[
x = 80
\]

The other intercepted arc is \(80^\circ\).
</details>

### Guided Problem 3

An angle formed by intersecting chords uses intercepted arcs of \(45^\circ\) and \(135^\circ\). Is the angle acute, right, or obtuse?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the angle first, then classify it.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\[
\frac{1}{2}(45^\circ + 135^\circ) = \frac{1}{2}(180^\circ) = 90^\circ
\]

The angle is a right angle.
</details>

## Mini-Quiz

Try these without looking back.

1. What formula finds an angle formed by two chords intersecting inside a circle?
2. If the intercepted arcs are \(110^\circ\) and \(50^\circ\), what is the angle?
3. If the angle is \(75^\circ\) and one intercepted arc is \(90^\circ\), what is the other intercepted arc?
4. True or false: For intersecting chords inside a circle, subtract the arcs and take half.

<details>
<summary>Reveal mini-quiz answers</summary>

1. \(m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(\text{arc 1} + \text{arc 2})\)
2. \(80^\circ\)
3. \(60^\circ\), because \(2(75) = 150\) and \(150 - 90 = 60\)
4. False. Use half the sum.
</details>

- Use vertical angles to choose the correct opposite arc pair.
- Explain the difference between inside-chord angle rules and exterior angle rules.
