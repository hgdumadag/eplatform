## What You Should Already Know

Before starting, make sure these facts feel familiar:

- A **radius** goes from the center to a point on the circle.
- A **chord** joins two points on the circle.
- A **tangent** touches a circle at exactly one point.
- A radius drawn to a tangent point is perpendicular to the tangent.
- A sector with central angle \(\theta\) has area \(\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2\).

### Pre-check / Readiness Quiz

Try these before reading the rest of the lesson.

1. What fraction of a circle is a \(90^\circ\) sector?
2. What is the area of a circle with radius \(8\)?
3. If a tangent touches a circle at \(A\), what is the measure of the angle between the tangent and radius \(OA\)?
4. If a right triangle has legs \(6\) and \(6\), what is the length of its hypotenuse?

<details>
<summary>Reveal pre-check answers</summary>

1. A \(90^\circ\) sector is \(\frac{1}{4}\) of a circle.
2. The area is \(64\pi\) square units.
3. The angle is \(90^\circ\).
4. The hypotenuse is \(\sqrt{6^2 + 6^2} = 6\sqrt{2}\).

If more than one item felt uncertain, review circle parts, sector area, and the Pythagorean theorem before continuing.
</details>

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quarter circle | A \(90^\circ\) sector, or one-fourth of a full circle |
| Chord | A segment with both endpoints on the circle |
| Tangent | A line or segment that touches the circle at exactly one point |
| Sector | A region bounded by two radii and an arc |
| Segment region | The region between a chord and its arc |
| Design requirement | A measurement condition the diagram must satisfy |

> [!TARGET] Target Skill
>
> Read the diagram first. Then choose the angle, length, or area relationship that matches the required measurement.

## The Design

The task uses a quarter-circle logo panel. The center is \(O\), the radius is \(8\) cm, and points \(A\) and \(B\) are endpoints of the quarter circle.

![Quarter-circle design with radius, chord, tangent, and sector labels](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-quarter-circle-geometry-task/images/quarter-circle-design-overview.svg)

The design includes:

- radii \(OA\) and \(OB\),
- chord \(AB\),
- a tangent at \(A\),
- the \(90^\circ\) sector \(AOB\),
- the smaller region between chord \(AB\) and the arc.

## Main Concept 1: Angle Requirements

In a quarter circle, the central angle is:

$$
\angle AOB = 90^\circ
$$

The tangent at \(A\) is perpendicular to radius \(OA\), so:

$$
\angle OAT = 90^\circ
$$

If chord \(AB\) joins the endpoints of the quarter circle, triangle \(AOB\) has two equal radii and a right angle at the center. That means triangle \(AOB\) is an isosceles right triangle.

So the base angles are:

$$
\angle OAB = \angle OBA = \frac{180^\circ - 90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ
$$

![Angle map showing tangent-radius and chord relationships](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-quarter-circle-geometry-task/images/tangent-chord-angle-map.svg)

> [!TIP] Fast Angle Check
>
> In this design, remember the pattern \(90^\circ, 45^\circ, 45^\circ\). The tangent gives another \(90^\circ\) at the point of tangency.

## Main Concept 2: Length Requirements

The chord \(AB\) is the hypotenuse of right triangle \(AOB\).

If \(OA = 8\) cm and \(OB = 8\) cm, then:

$$
AB^2 = 8^2 + 8^2
$$

$$
AB^2 = 128
$$

$$
AB = \sqrt{128} = 8\sqrt{2}
$$

So the chord is \(8\sqrt{2}\) cm, or about \(11.31\) cm.

### Worked Length Example

**Problem:** A designer scales the same quarter-circle pattern to radius \(6\) cm. Find the chord joining the two endpoints of the quarter circle.

**Step 1: Use the two radii as right-triangle legs.**

`6` and `6` are the legs.

**Step 2: Apply the Pythagorean theorem.**

$$
c^2 = 6^2 + 6^2 = 72
$$

**Step 3: Simplify.**

$$
c = \sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}
$$

The chord is \(6\sqrt{2}\) cm.

## Main Concept 3: Area Requirements

A quarter-circle sector is one-fourth of a full circle.

For radius \(8\) cm:

$$
\text{sector area} = \frac{90^\circ}{360^\circ}\pi(8)^2
$$

$$
\text{sector area} = \frac{1}{4}\cdot 64\pi = 16\pi
$$

The right triangle \(AOB\) has legs \(8\) and \(8\), so:

$$
\text{triangle area} = \frac{1}{2}(8)(8) = 32
$$

The curved segment between the chord and arc is:

$$
16\pi - 32
$$

That is about \(18.27\) square centimeters.

![Sector area breakdown for a quarter-circle design](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-quarter-circle-geometry-task/images/sector-area-breakdown.svg)

> [!WARNING] Common Area Trap
>
> Do not use the full-circle area when the design only asks for the quarter sector. A \(90^\circ\) sector uses \(\frac{1}{4}\pi r^2\).

## Guided Practice

### Guided Problem 1: Angle

In a quarter-circle design, \(OA\) and \(OB\) are radii and \(AB\) is the chord between the endpoints. What is \(\angle OAB\)?

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Triangle \(AOB\) has central angle \(90^\circ\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The radii are equal, so the other two angles are equal.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

\(\angle OAB = 45^\circ\).
</details>

### Guided Problem 2: Length

A quarter-circle design has radius \(10\) cm. Find the chord joining the two endpoints of the quarter circle.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The chord is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs \(10\) and \(10\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Use \(c^2 = 10^2 + 10^2\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

\(c = \sqrt{200} = 10\sqrt{2}\) cm.
</details>

### Guided Problem 3: Area

A quarter-circle design has radius \(12\) cm. Find the area of the sector.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

A quarter circle is one-fourth of a full circle.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Use \(\frac{1}{4}\pi(12)^2\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The sector area is \(36\pi\) square centimeters.
</details>

## Design Checklist

Before moving to the exams, you should be able to:

- Label the radius, chord, tangent, and sector in a diagram.
- Use \(90^\circ\) for the quarter-circle central angle.
- Use perpendicular radii and tangents to find angle requirements.
- Use the Pythagorean theorem for the endpoint chord.
- Use \(\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\pi r^2\) for sector area.
- Subtract triangle area from sector area when the curved segment is required.

> [!PRACTICE] Exam Plan
>
> Use the practice exam for quick recognition and setup. Use the assessment when you can move between angle, length, and area requirements without mixing the formulas.
