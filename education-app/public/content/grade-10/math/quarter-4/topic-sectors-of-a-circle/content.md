## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sector | A region of a circle bounded by two radii and the arc between them |
| Central angle | An angle whose vertex is at the center of the circle |
| Radius | The distance from the center to the circle |
| Arc | A connected part of the circle's edge |
| Sector area | The area inside a sector |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> A sector is a fraction of a circle. If the central angle is \(\theta^\circ\), then the sector is \(\frac{\theta}{360}\) of the full circle.

## 1. What Is a Sector?

A sector looks like a slice of pizza or a slice of pie. It has:

- two radii as straight sides;
- one arc as the curved side;
- one central angle between the two radii.

![Sector parts labeled with two radii, arc, and central angle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-sectors-of-a-circle/images/sector-parts.svg)

The central angle controls how large the sector is. A larger central angle gives a larger fraction of the circle.

> [!CHECK] Quick Check
>
> A region bounded by a chord and an arc is called a segment, not a sector. A sector must use two radii.

## 2. Think Fraction First

A full circle is \(360^\circ\). So a sector with central angle \(\theta^\circ\) uses this fraction:

$$
\text{fraction of circle} = \frac{\theta}{360}
$$

![Common sectors shown as fractions of a full circle](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-sectors-of-a-circle/images/fraction-of-circle.svg)

| Central angle | Fraction of circle | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| \(90^\circ\) | \(\frac{90}{360} = \frac{1}{4}\) | one quarter |
| \(120^\circ\) | \(\frac{120}{360} = \frac{1}{3}\) | one third |
| \(180^\circ\) | \(\frac{180}{360} = \frac{1}{2}\) | one half |
| \(270^\circ\) | \(\frac{270}{360} = \frac{3}{4}\) | three quarters |

> [!TIP] Fast Decision
>
> Do not start by memorizing many separate sector formulas. First ask, "What fraction of the circle is shaded?"

## 3. Sector Area Formula

The whole circle has area:

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

The sector is only a fraction of that area:

$$
\text{sector area} = \frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2
$$

where:

- \(\theta\) is the central angle in degrees;
- \(r\) is the radius.

![Workflow for calculating sector area from angle and radius](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-4/topic-sectors-of-a-circle/images/sector-area-workflow.svg)

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Quarter-Circle Sector

**Problem:** Find the area of a sector with central angle \(90^\circ\) and radius \(8\).

**Solution:**

First find the fraction of the circle.

$$
\frac{90}{360} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

Now find the full circle area.

$$
\pi r^2 = \pi(8^2) = 64\pi
$$

Take one fourth of the full circle area.

$$
\text{sector area} = \frac{1}{4}(64\pi) = 16\pi
$$

**Answer:** \(16\pi\) square units

### Example 2: Use the Formula Directly

**Problem:** Find the area of a sector with central angle \(120^\circ\) and radius \(6\).

**Solution:**

$$
\text{sector area} = \frac{120}{360}\pi(6^2)
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{3}\cdot 36\pi = 12\pi
$$

**Answer:** \(12\pi\) square units

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> The angle fraction is \(\frac{\theta}{360}\), not \(\frac{360}{\theta}\). A \(60^\circ\) sector should be smaller than the whole circle, so its fraction is \(\frac{60}{360} = \frac{1}{6}\).

## Guided Practice

### Guided Problem 1

A sector has central angle \(45^\circ\). What fraction of the circle is it?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use \(\frac{\theta}{360}\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\[
\frac{45}{360} = \frac{1}{8}
\]

The sector is \(\frac{1}{8}\) of the circle.
</details>

### Guided Problem 2

A sector has central angle \(180^\circ\) and radius \(5\). Find its exact area.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

A \(180^\circ\) sector is half of the circle.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Full circle area:

\[
\pi(5^2) = 25\pi
\]

Half of that:

\[
\frac{1}{2}(25\pi) = \frac{25\pi}{2}
\]

The sector area is \(\frac{25\pi}{2}\) square units.
</details>

### Guided Problem 3

A sector has central angle \(72^\circ\) and radius \(10\). Find its exact area.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

First simplify \(\frac{72}{360}\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\[
\frac{72}{360} = \frac{1}{5}
\]

Full circle area:

\[
\pi(10^2) = 100\pi
\]

Sector area:

\[
\frac{1}{5}(100\pi) = 20\pi
\]

The sector area is \(20\pi\) square units.
</details>

## Strategy: Fraction-of-Circle Reasoning

Use this four-step path:

1. Identify the central angle \(\theta\).
2. Write the sector fraction \(\frac{\theta}{360}\).
3. Find the full circle area \(\pi r^2\).
4. Multiply the fraction by the full circle area.

| Question asks for | Best first move |
|---|---|
| Sector fraction | Simplify \(\frac{\theta}{360}\) |
| Exact sector area | Keep \(\pi\) in the answer |
| Decimal area | Calculate after multiplying |
| Missing radius or angle | Write the sector area equation first |

## Final Checklist

You are ready for practice when you can:

- define a sector using two radii and an arc;
- identify the central angle of a sector;
- explain why \(\frac{\theta}{360}\) gives the fraction of the circle;
- calculate full circle area using \(\pi r^2\);
- multiply the circle area by the sector fraction;
- keep exact answers in terms of \(\pi\) when requested.
