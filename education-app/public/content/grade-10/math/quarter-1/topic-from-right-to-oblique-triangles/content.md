## What Is an Oblique Triangle?

An **oblique triangle** is any triangle with **no right angle**. That means it has no 90-degree angle.

There are two common types:

- **Acute oblique triangle:** all three angles are less than 90 degrees.
- **Obtuse oblique triangle:** one angle is greater than 90 degrees.

![Acute and obtuse oblique triangles](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-from-right-to-oblique-triangles/images/oblique-types.svg)

> [!CHECK] Mini Check
>
> A triangle has angles 41 degrees, 64 degrees, and 75 degrees. Is it right or oblique?

<details>
<summary>Reveal answer</summary>

It is an acute oblique triangle. None of the angles is 90 degrees, and all angles are less than 90 degrees.

</details>

## Naming the Given Information

Triangle-solving cases are shorthand labels. Each letter tells whether a known part is an **Angle** or a **Side**.

| Case | Meaning | How to recognize it |
|---|---|---|
| ASA | Angle-Side-Angle | Two angles and the side between them |
| AAS | Angle-Angle-Side | Two angles and a side not between them |
| SSA | Side-Side-Angle | Two sides and a non-included angle |
| SAS | Side-Angle-Side | Two sides and the angle between them |
| SSS | Side-Side-Side | All three side lengths |

![Map of common triangle given cases](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-from-right-to-oblique-triangles/images/given-case-map.svg)

### The Word "Included"

The **included** part sits between the other two known parts.

- In **ASA**, the known side is between the two known angles.
- In **SAS**, the known angle is between the two known sides.
- In **AAS** and **SSA**, the last known part is not between the other two.

> [!TIP] Read Diagrams Like a Detective
>
> Mark every known side and known angle first. Then walk around the triangle in order. The order of known parts usually tells you the case.

## Visual Cases: ASA, AAS, and SSA

These three cases often connect to Law of Sines lessons later. For now, your job is to identify the pattern.

![ASA AAS and SSA triangle diagrams](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-from-right-to-oblique-triangles/images/asa-aas-ssa.svg)

### Example 1: ASA

A triangle has angles `A = 52 degrees` and `B = 68 degrees`. The side `AB` between those two angles is 14 cm.

Known parts:

- angle
- included side
- angle

So the case is **ASA**.

### Example 2: AAS

A triangle has angles `A = 44 degrees` and `B = 73 degrees`. A side opposite one of those angles is 12 cm.

Known parts:

- angle
- angle
- non-included side

So the case is **AAS**.

### Example 3: SSA

A triangle has sides `a = 8` and `b = 11`, and angle `A = 39 degrees`.

Known parts:

- side
- side
- non-included angle

So the case is **SSA**.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> SSA is not the same as SAS. In SAS, the known angle is squeezed between the two known sides. In SSA, the known angle is not between them.

## Visual Cases: SAS and SSS

These cases often connect to Law of Cosines lessons later.

![SAS and SSS triangle diagrams](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-from-right-to-oblique-triangles/images/sas-sss.svg)

### Example 4: SAS

A triangle has two sides measuring 7 m and 10 m. The angle between those two sides is 42 degrees.

The known angle is between the two known sides, so the case is **SAS**.

### Example 5: SSS

A triangle has side lengths 6 cm, 9 cm, and 11 cm.

All three sides are known, so the case is **SSS**.

## Decision Guide

Use this guide before choosing a formula.

| If the triangle has... | Classify it as... | First thought |
|---|---|---|
| one 90-degree angle | right triangle | Right-triangle trigonometry may fit |
| no 90-degree angle | oblique triangle | Identify the given case |
| two angles and one side | ASA or AAS | Find the third angle first |
| two sides and a non-included angle | SSA | Check carefully; it can be ambiguous |
| two sides and the included angle | SAS | Law of Cosines may be useful later |
| three sides | SSS | Law of Cosines may be useful later |
