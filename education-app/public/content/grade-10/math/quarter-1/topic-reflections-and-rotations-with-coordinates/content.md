## What You Should Already Know

Before studying reflections and rotations, make sure these ideas feel familiar:

- An ordered pair is written as \((x, y)\).
- The x-coordinate tells left or right movement from the origin.
- The y-coordinate tells up or down movement from the origin.
- A **preimage** is the original figure.
- An **image** is the figure after a transformation.
- A rigid transformation preserves size and shape.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transformation | A movement or change of a figure on the plane |
| Preimage | The original point or figure before a transformation |
| Image | The new point or figure after a transformation |
| Reflection | A flip across a line, called the line of reflection |
| Rotation | A turn around a fixed point, called the center of rotation |
| Origin | The point \((0, 0)\) |
| Counterclockwise | Turning in the opposite direction of clock hands |
| Corresponding points | Matching points before and after a transformation, such as \(A\) and \(A'\) |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> Reflections and rotations are rigid transformations. The figure changes position, but its side lengths and angle measures stay the same.

## Main Concept Explanation

### 1. Reflection Rules

A reflection flips a point across a line. On the coordinate plane, common reflection rules are:

| Reflection | Coordinate Rule |
|---|---|
| Across the x-axis | \((x, y) \rightarrow (x, -y)\) |
| Across the y-axis | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-x, y)\) |
| Across the origin | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-x, -y)\) |
| Across the line \(y = x\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (y, x)\) |

![Reflection across the x-axis and y-axis](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-reflections-and-rotations-with-coordinates/images/inline-02-reflection-across-the-x-axis-and-y-axis.svg)

### 2. Rotation Rules About the Origin

A rotation turns a point around a center. In this lesson, the center is the origin.

Common counterclockwise rotation rules:

| Rotation about \((0, 0)\) | Coordinate Rule |
|---|---|
| \(90°\) counterclockwise | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-y, x)\) |
| \(180°\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-x, -y)\) |
| \(270°\) counterclockwise | \((x, y) \rightarrow (y, -x)\) |
| \(360°\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (x, y)\) |

![Rotating a point around the origin](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-reflections-and-rotations-with-coordinates/images/inline-03-rotating-a-point-around-the-origin.svg)

## Rule Box / Formula Box

Use this table as your quick reference.

| Transformation | Rule | What changes? |
|---|---|---|
| Reflect over x-axis | \((x, y) \rightarrow (x, -y)\) | y changes sign |
| Reflect over y-axis | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-x, y)\) | x changes sign |
| Reflect over origin | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-x, -y)\) | both signs change |
| Reflect over \(y = x\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (y, x)\) | coordinates switch places |
| Rotate \(90°\) CCW | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-y, x)\) | switch, then make old y opposite |
| Rotate \(180°\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (-x, -y)\) | both signs change |
| Rotate \(270°\) CCW | \((x, y) \rightarrow (y, -x)\) | switch, then make old x opposite |

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Reflecting One Point

**Problem:** Reflect \(A(5, -2)\) across the y-axis.

**Solution:**

Across the y-axis, use \((x, y) \rightarrow (-x, y)\).

\[
(5, -2) \rightarrow (-5, -2)
\]

**Answer:** \(A'(-5, -2)\)

### Example 2: Rotating One Point

**Problem:** Rotate \(B(-3, 4)\) \(90°\) counterclockwise about the origin.

**Solution:**

For \(90°\) counterclockwise, use \((x, y) \rightarrow (-y, x)\).

\[
(-3, 4) \rightarrow (-4, -3)
\]

**Answer:** \(B'(-4, -3)\)

### Example 3: Transforming a Triangle

**Problem:** Triangle \(ABC\) has \(A(1, 1)\), \(B(4, 1)\), and \(C(2, 3)\). Reflect it across the x-axis.

![Triangle reflection across the x-axis](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-reflections-and-rotations-with-coordinates/images/inline-04-triangle-reflection-across-the-x-axis.svg)

**Solution:**

Reflecting across the x-axis keeps x the same and changes the sign of y.

| Point | Rule | Image |
|---|---|---|
| \(A(1, 1)\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (x, -y)\) | \(A'(1, -1)\) |
| \(B(4, 1)\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (x, -y)\) | \(B'(4, -1)\) |
| \(C(2, 3)\) | \((x, y) \rightarrow (x, -y)\) | \(C'(2, -3)\) |

**Answer:** \(A'(1, -1)\), \(B'(4, -1)\), \(C'(2, -3)\)

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Trap 1: Mixing up x-axis and y-axis reflections
>
> Reflecting across the x-axis changes the y-coordinate. Reflecting across the y-axis changes the x-coordinate. The axis that acts like the mirror stays in place.

> [!WARNING] Trap 2: Rotating 90° by only switching coordinates
>
> A \(90°\) counterclockwise rotation is not just \((x, y) \rightarrow (y, x)\). The correct rule is \((x, y) \rightarrow (-y, x)\).

> [!WARNING] Trap 3: Forgetting to transform every vertex
>
> For a triangle or polygon, apply the same rule to each vertex. One missed vertex changes the whole image.

## Error Analysis

A student rotates \(M(4, -9)\) \(90°\) counterclockwise and writes:

\[
(4, -9) \rightarrow (-9, 4)
\]

The answer is incorrect.

<details>
<summary>Identify the mistake</summary>

The student switched the coordinates but did not apply the sign rule correctly. For \(90°\) counterclockwise, \((x, y) \rightarrow (-y, x)\). Since \(y = -9\), \(-y = 9\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal corrected solution</summary>

\[
(4, -9) \rightarrow (9, 4)
\]

So the correct image is \(M'(9, 4)\).
</details>

## Self-Explanation Prompts

Use these prompts to check whether you can explain the rules, not just memorize them.

1. How can you tell from the coordinates whether a point was reflected across the x-axis?
2. Why does reflecting across \(y = x\) switch the coordinates?
3. How are a \(180°\) rotation about the origin and a reflection across the origin similar?
4. Why should you transform each vertex separately when working with a polygon?

<details>
<summary>Reveal sample responses</summary>

1. The x-coordinate stays the same and the y-coordinate changes sign.
2. The line \(y = x\) contains points where both coordinates are equal, so flipping across it swaps the horizontal and vertical distances.
3. Both send \((x, y)\) to \((-x, -y)\), so the coordinate result is the same.
4. A polygon is located by its vertices, so each matching image vertex must follow the same rule.
</details>

## Extension Challenge

Point \(N(-2, 5)\) is reflected across the y-axis, then the image is rotated \(90°\) counterclockwise about the origin. What is the final point?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Do the transformations one at a time. Use the result of the reflection as the input for the rotation.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Full solution</summary>

Reflect across the y-axis:

\[
(-2, 5) \rightarrow (2, 5)
\]

Rotate \(90°\) counterclockwise:

\[
(2, 5) \rightarrow (-5, 2)
\]

The final point is \((-5, 2)\).
</details>

## Final Summary

Reflections and rotations move figures without changing their size or shape. Reflections flip across a line, while rotations turn around a point. The most reliable strategy is to choose the correct coordinate rule, apply it carefully to each point, and compare the preimage and image coordinates to explain what changed.
