# Trigonometry - Lesson 8: Bearings and Triangle Diagrams

> [!GOAL] Learning Goal
>
> By the end of this lesson, you should be able to translate bearing descriptions into clear oblique triangle diagrams before doing any solving.

## Study Setup

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Time | About 40 minutes |
| Main skill | Drawing triangle diagrams from bearings |
| Materials | Compass rose handout, ruler, protractor |
| Focus | Orientation and angle placement, not computation |

## Opening Navigation Check

Imagine this path:

1. A hiker starts at Base A.
2. The hiker walks 4 km on a bearing of 060° to Camp B.
3. From Camp B, the hiker walks 3 km on a bearing of 130° to Lookout C.

Before drawing, ask yourself:

- Which way is north at point A?
- Which way is north at point B?
- Where does the direction change happen?
- Which three points will form the triangle?

The answer is not a number yet. The first goal is a diagram that shows the route accurately.

## Bearings in One Picture

A bearing is a three-digit direction angle measured clockwise from north.

![A 060 degree bearing measured clockwise from a north line](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-bearings-and-triangle-diagrams/images/compass-bearing-060.svg)

> [!IMPORTANT] Bearing Rule
>
> Always start at north. Turn clockwise. Then draw the ray in that direction.

## What Each Bearing Statement Gives You

| Statement part | What to draw |
|---|---|
| "From A" | Start at point A |
| "to B" | End the ray at point B |
| "6 km" | Label the side length |
| "bearing of 045°" | Measure 45° clockwise from north at the starting point |
| "then from B" | Draw a new north line at B before measuring the next bearing |

### Compass Quadrant Guide

| Bearing range | General direction |
|---|---|
| 000° to 090° | Northeast side |
| 090° to 180° | Southeast side |
| 180° to 270° | Southwest side |
| 270° to 360° | Northwest side |

If a bearing is exactly 090°, 180°, or 270°, it points east, south, or west.

## Drawing Checklist

Use this order every time:

1. Plot and label the starting point.
2. Draw a short north line at the point where the bearing begins.
3. Measure the bearing clockwise from that north line.
4. Draw the travel ray or segment.
5. Label the distance and the endpoint.
6. If the route continues, draw a new north line at the new point.
7. Connect the start and final point if the direct distance is part of the triangle.

> [!TIP] Protractor Habit
>
> Put the protractor's center on the point where the bearing begins. Align 0° with the north line, then mark the clockwise angle.

## Example 1: One Bearing, One Segment

**Problem:** From point A, point B is 7 km away on a bearing of 040°. Draw the diagram.

**Diagram moves:**

1. Draw point A.
2. Draw a north line at A.
3. From north, turn clockwise 40°.
4. Draw segment AB in that direction.
5. Label AB as 7 km.

**Check:** Since 040° is between 000° and 090°, segment AB should point generally northeast.

## Example 2: Two Bearings From the Same Point

**Problem:** From station P, tower Q is 5 km away on a bearing of 025°. Tower R is 8 km away on a bearing of 105°. Draw triangle PQR and mark the included angle.

![Two bearings from the same point creating an included angle at P](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-bearings-and-triangle-diagrams/images/two-bearings-same-point.svg)

**Diagram moves:**

1. Draw point P and a north line at P.
2. Draw PQ on a bearing of 025° and label it 5 km.
3. Draw PR on a bearing of 105° and label it 8 km.
4. Connect Q to R to complete the triangle.
5. Mark the included angle at P because both known sides meet at P.

The angle between the two rays is:

$$105^\circ - 025^\circ = 80^\circ$$

**Check:** The 80° angle belongs inside the triangle at P, not beside the north line.

## Example 3: A Two-Leg Route

**Problem:** A boat travels from A to B for 6 km on a bearing of 070°. From B, it travels 4 km on a bearing of 145° to C. Draw the route triangle.

![A two-leg route with separate north lines at A and B](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-bearings-and-triangle-diagrams/images/two-leg-route-local-north.svg)

**Diagram moves:**

1. Draw A and a north line at A.
2. Draw AB on bearing 070° and label it 6 km.
3. At B, draw a new north line.
4. Draw BC on bearing 145° and label it 4 km.
5. Connect A to C. Segment AC is the direct distance from the start to the final point.

> [!WARNING] Common Error
>
> Do not measure the second bearing from the north line at A. A bearing that starts "from B" must be measured from a north line at B.

## Included Angles and Back Bearings

Sometimes the angle inside the triangle is not the same as the second bearing number. You may need to notice the direction of the previous side when viewed from the turning point.

![Included angle at a turn point found using a back bearing](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-1/topic-bearings-and-triangle-diagrams/images/included-angle-back-bearing.svg)

For a route A to B to C:

- The side BA points back toward A.
- The direction from B back to A is the back bearing of AB.
- The included angle at B is the angle between BA and BC.

Back bearing rule:

$$\text{back bearing} = \text{bearing} + 180^\circ \quad \text{or} \quad \text{bearing} - 180^\circ$$

Choose the result from 000° to 360°.

### Worked Turn-Angle Check

If AB has bearing 060°, then the back bearing from B to A is:

$$060^\circ + 180^\circ = 240^\circ$$

If BC has bearing 130°, then the smaller angle between BA and BC is:

$$240^\circ - 130^\circ = 110^\circ$$

So the included angle at B is 110°.

## Mini Drill

Draw each diagram on paper. Do not solve for unknown side lengths.

1. From A, B is 9 km away on bearing 030°.
2. From P, Q is 4 km away on bearing 015° and R is 6 km away on bearing 095°.
3. A ship travels from X to Y for 10 km on bearing 120°, then from Y to Z for 7 km on bearing 210°.
4. A drone flies from M to N for 5 km on bearing 300°, then from N to O for 8 km on bearing 045°.

2. Both rays start at P; the included angle is at P.

## Diagram Quality Checklist

Before you submit a diagram, check for these features:

- Every point is labeled.
- Every given distance is placed on the correct segment.
- Every bearing is measured clockwise from a north line.
- A new north line is drawn at every new starting point.
- The direct distance is shown as the third side when the route forms a triangle.
- The included angle is inside the triangle and at the vertex where the two relevant sides meet.
