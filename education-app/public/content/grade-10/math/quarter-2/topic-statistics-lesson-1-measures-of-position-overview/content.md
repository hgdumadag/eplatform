## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ordered data | Data values arranged from least to greatest |
| Measure of position | A value that describes where a data value or cut point is located in an ordered data set |
| Quartiles | Three cut points, \(Q_1\), \(Q_2\), and \(Q_3\), that divide ordered data into four parts |
| Deciles | Nine cut points, \(D_1\) through \(D_9\), that divide ordered data into ten parts |
| Percentiles | Ninety-nine cut points, \(P_1\) through \(P_{99}\), that divide ordered data into one hundred parts |
| Median | The middle value; it is also \(Q_2\), \(D_5\), and \(P_{50}\) |
| Locator | A formula result that estimates the position of a quartile, decile, or percentile in the ordered list |
| Percentile rank | The percent of data values at or below a given value |

> [!TARGET] Target Skill
>
> Read the data in order first. Then decide whether the question asks for quarters, tenths, hundredths, or the meaning of a position.

## Visual Introduction: Position Comes From Order

A measure of position is not just another average. It tells where a value sits after the data are arranged.

![Ordered data line showing how positions are read from least to greatest](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-1-measures-of-position-overview/images/lesson-visual.svg)

Use this class score data set throughout the lesson:

$$
42,\ 48,\ 55,\ 61,\ 64,\ 68,\ 72,\ 75,\ 80,\ 84,\ 88,\ 92
$$

The list has \(n = 12\) scores. Low positions are near the beginning. High positions are near the end. The middle position is near the center of the ordered list.

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> Quartiles, deciles, and percentiles all describe **relative position**. The difference is how many equal parts the ordered data are divided into.

## Three Ways To Divide Position

| Measure | Divides ordered data into | Important examples | Everyday meaning |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Quartiles | 4 parts | \(Q_1, Q_2, Q_3\) | lower quarter, middle, upper quarter |
| Deciles | 10 parts | \(D_1, D_5, D_9\) | tenths of the data |
| Percentiles | 100 parts | \(P_{25}, P_{50}, P_{75}\) | percent at or below a position |

![Quartile cut points splitting an ordered data set into four equal position groups](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-1-measures-of-position-overview/images/quartile-map.svg)

Notice the overlap:

- \(Q_1\) is the same position as \(P_{25}\).
- \(Q_2\) is the median, \(D_5\), and \(P_{50}\).
- \(Q_3\) is the same position as \(P_{75}\).

## Locator Formulas

Many Grade 10 statistics problems use a locator to estimate where a position measure falls in an ordered list.

| Measure | Locator |
|---|---|
| \(Q_k\) | \(L = \frac{k(n+1)}{4}\), where \(k = 1,2,3\) |
| \(D_k\) | \(L = \frac{k(n+1)}{10}\), where \(k = 1,2,\ldots,9\) |
| \(P_k\) | \(L = \frac{k(n+1)}{100}\), where \(k = 1,2,\ldots,99\) |

![Locator formula flow from choose measure to compute position to interpret value](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-1-measures-of-position-overview/images/locator-formula-flow.svg)

If \(L\) is a whole number, use the value in that position. If \(L\) is not a whole number, the position is between two data values. Your teacher or textbook may ask you to interpolate; in an overview lesson, the key is to recognize the two neighboring positions and explain the meaning.

> [!TIP] Quick Translation
>
> The subscript tells the part. \(Q_3\) means the third quartile, \(D_7\) means the seventh decile, and \(P_{80}\) means the eightieth percentile.

## Worked Example

**Problem:** Use the ordered scores below to locate and interpret \(Q_2\), \(Q_3\), and \(P_{75}\).

$$
42,\ 48,\ 55,\ 61,\ 64,\ 68,\ 72,\ 75,\ 80,\ 84,\ 88,\ 92
$$

**Step 1: Count the values.**

There are \(n = 12\) scores.

**Step 2: Locate \(Q_2\).**

\(Q_2\) is the median. With 12 values, the median is between the 6th and 7th values.

$$
Q_2 = \frac{68 + 72}{2} = 70
$$

So the middle score is \(70\).

**Step 3: Locate \(Q_3\).**

Use the locator for \(Q_3\):

$$
L = \frac{3(n+1)}{4} = \frac{3(13)}{4} = 9.75
$$

The position \(9.75\) is between the 9th value, \(80\), and the 10th value, \(84\). So \(Q_3\) is near the upper end of the data and lies between \(80\) and \(84\).

**Step 4: Connect \(Q_3\) and \(P_{75}\).**

The third quartile is the same position as the 75th percentile.

$$
Q_3 = P_{75}
$$

**Interpretation:** About 75% of the class scores are at or below the third quartile position. In this data set, that position is between \(80\) and \(84\).

> [!EXAMPLE] Complete Answer
>
> \(Q_2 = 70\). \(Q_3\), or \(P_{75}\), is between \(80\) and \(84\), so about three-fourths of the scores are at or below that upper-quarter position.

## Answer Key with Explanations

1. \(n = 10\). There are 10 values.
2. \(Q_2 = \frac{26 + 29}{2} = 27.5\), because the median is between the 5th and 6th values.
3. \(Q_1\) is the same position as \(P_{25}\).
4. \(Q_3\) is the same position as \(P_{75}\).
5. \(L = \frac{4(n+1)}{10} = \frac{4(11)}{10} = 4.4\).
6. \(D_4\) falls between the 4th and 5th positions, or between \(22\) and \(26\).
7. \(P_{80}\) is the position where about 80% of the data values are at or below that point.
8. \(P_{80}\) is a position, not automatically the data value 80. You must use the ordered data to find the value at that position.

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Misconception 1: "Percentile means percent correct."
>
> A percentile describes position in a group. A score at \(P_{80}\) is not automatically an 80% score.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 2: "You can use the original order."
>
> Measures of position require ordered data. If the list is not arranged from least to greatest, the positions can be wrong.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 3: "Quartiles, deciles, and percentiles are unrelated."
>
> They overlap. For example, \(Q_2 = D_5 = P_{50}\), and \(Q_3 = P_{75}\).

## Final Summary

Measures of position describe where values stand in an ordered data set. Quartiles use fourths, deciles use tenths, and percentiles use hundredths. The strongest answers do three things: order the data, locate the correct position, and explain the meaning in the original situation.
