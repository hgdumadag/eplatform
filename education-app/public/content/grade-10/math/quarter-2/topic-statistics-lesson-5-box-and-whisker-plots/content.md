> How can five carefully chosen values summarize the spread and center of an entire data set?

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum | The least value in the data set. |
| Q1, or first quartile | The median of the lower half of the ordered data. About 25% of the data are at or below this value. |
| Median, or Q2 | The middle value of the ordered data. About half the data are at or below this value. |
| Q3, or third quartile | The median of the upper half of the ordered data. About 75% of the data are at or below this value. |
| Maximum | The greatest value in the data set. |
| Five-number summary | The minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum written in order. |
| Box-and-whisker plot | A display that places the five-number summary on a number line. The box runs from Q1 to Q3, and the whiskers extend to the minimum and maximum. |

## Visual Introduction

A box-and-whisker plot is built from five landmarks.

![The five-number summary labeled on a box plot](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-5-box-and-whisker-plots/images/five-number-summary-anatomy.svg)

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> A box plot does not show every individual data value. It shows how the data are divided into quarters using the minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum.

Each section of a box plot represents about 25% of the data:

| Section | Values represented |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum to Q1 | Lowest quarter of the data |
| Q1 to median | Second quarter of the data |
| Median to Q3 | Third quarter of the data |
| Q3 to maximum | Highest quarter of the data |

## Main Concept Explanation

### 1. Find the Five-Number Summary

Use ordered data. For an odd number of values, the median is the middle value. Then find Q1 from the lower half and Q3 from the upper half, leaving out the overall median.

![Ordered data split into lower half, median, and upper half](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-5-box-and-whisker-plots/images/quartile-split-ordered-data.svg)

For the reading minutes data:

$$
10, 12, 15, 18, 20, 21, 24, 26, 30
$$

| Part | Values | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum | first value | 10 |
| Q1 | median of $10, 12, 15, 18$ | $(12 + 15) \div 2 = 13.5$ |
| Median | middle value | 20 |
| Q3 | median of $21, 24, 26, 30$ | $(24 + 26) \div 2 = 25$ |
| Maximum | last value | 30 |

The five-number summary is:

$$
10,\ 13.5,\ 20,\ 25,\ 30
$$

### 2. Draw the Plot on a Scale

A box plot must use a reasonable number line scale. The distances matter.

![Construction steps from number line to completed box plot](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-5-box-and-whisker-plots/images/box-plot-construction-steps.svg)

Follow this routine:

1. Draw a number line that includes the minimum and maximum.
2. Mark the minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum.
3. Draw a box from Q1 to Q3.
4. Draw a vertical line inside the box at the median.
5. Draw whiskers from Q1 to the minimum and from Q3 to the maximum.

> [!TIP] Plotting Habit
>
> Always place the values on a scaled number line before drawing the box. A box plot with uneven or unlabeled spacing can be misleading.

### 3. Interpret the Shape

The box shows the middle 50% of the data. The whiskers show the lower and upper spread outside the box.

![Two box plots comparing symmetric and right-skewed spread](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-5-box-and-whisker-plots/images/box-plot-shape-comparison.svg)

Ask:

- Where is the median inside the box?
- Which whisker is longer?
- Is the middle 50% tightly packed or spread out?
- Does one side of the data stretch farther than the other?

> [!WARNING] Interpretation Trap
>
> A longer section of a box plot does not mean more data points are in that section. Each quarter contains about the same fraction of the data, but the values may be more spread out.

## Rule Box / Formula Box

| Task | Move |
| --- | --- |
| Prepare the data | Order the values from least to greatest. |
| Find the center | Locate the median. |
| Find the box edges | Find Q1 and Q3 from the lower and upper halves. |
| Find the whisker ends | Use the minimum and maximum. |
| Draw accurately | Use a scaled number line and place all five values. |
| Interpret | Describe center, spread, and any longer side. |

**Five-number summary:**  

$$
\text{minimum},\ Q1,\ \text{median},\ Q3,\ \text{maximum}
$$

## Worked Example

**Problem:** Construct a box-and-whisker plot for the quiz scores below, then describe the spread.

$$
14, 18, 20, 21, 24, 27, 28, 30
$$

**Step 1: Confirm the data are ordered.**  
The values are already in increasing order.

**Step 2: Find the median.**  
There are 8 values, so the median is the average of the 4th and 5th values:

$$
(21 + 24) \div 2 = 22.5
$$

**Step 3: Find Q1 and Q3.**  
Lower half: $14, 18, 20, 21$

$$
Q1 = (18 + 20) \div 2 = 19
$$

Upper half: $24, 27, 28, 30$

$$
Q3 = (27 + 28) \div 2 = 27.5
$$

**Step 4: Write the five-number summary.**

$$
14,\ 19,\ 22.5,\ 27.5,\ 30
$$

**Step 5: Draw and interpret.**

![Worked example box plot for quiz scores](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-2/topic-statistics-lesson-5-box-and-whisker-plots/images/worked-example-quiz-scores.svg)

> [!EXAMPLE] Complete Answer
>
> The five-number summary is minimum 14, Q1 19, median 22.5, Q3 27.5, and maximum 30. The middle 50% of scores are from 19 to 27.5, and the lower whisker is longer than the upper whisker, so the lower scores are more spread out.

A classmate draws a box plot but places 10, 20, and 30 equally far apart while using no scale.

**Question:** What is the problem?

**Answer:** The plot may distort the spread. A box plot should use a number line scale so distances match the numerical values.

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Misconception 1: "The box contains all the data."
>
> The box contains the middle 50% of the data. The whiskers show the lower and upper quarters.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 2: "Longer means more data."
>
> A longer whisker or longer half of a box means the values are more spread out, not that there are more values there.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 3: "Any sketch is fine."
>
> A box plot should be drawn on a scaled number line. Without a scale, the display can exaggerate or hide spread.

## Error Analysis

A student writes:

> "The five-number summary is $10, 15, 20, 25, 30$, so I drew the box from 10 to 30."

**Find the mistake:** The student used the minimum and maximum as the ends of the box.

**Correct reasoning:** The box should run from Q1 to Q3, so it should go from 15 to 25. The whiskers extend from 15 down to 10 and from 25 up to 30.
