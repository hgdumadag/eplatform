# General Form of a Circle

> [!GOAL] Learning Goal
>
> You will learn how to recognize a circle equation when it is written in expanded form, identify its important coefficients, and decide whether an equation can represent a circle.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circle | The set of all points the same distance from a center |
| Center-radius form | `(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2` |
| General form | An expanded equation usually written as `x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0` |
| Coefficient | The number multiplying a variable, such as `D` in `Dx` |
| Constant | A number term without a variable, such as `F` |
| Expanded form | A form where squared binomials have been multiplied out |

## Main Idea

The general form of a circle is:

```text
x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0
```

Here is what each part does:

| Part | What to notice |
|---|---|
| `x^2` and `y^2` | Both must be present |
| Same coefficient | The coefficients of `x^2` and `y^2` must match |
| Positive squared terms | The squared terms usually appear with the same sign |
| `Dx` and `Ey` | These linear terms help determine the center |
| `F` | This constant helps determine the radius after completing the square |

> [!IMPORTANT] Recognition Rule
>
> A circle equation in general form has both `x^2` and `y^2`, and their coefficients are equal and nonzero. If the squared coefficients are different, the graph is not a circle.

## Circle or Not Circle?

![A comparison of equations that can represent circles and equations that cannot represent circles.](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-3/topic-general-form-of-a-circle/images/circle-vs-not-circle.svg)

Use these quick checks:

- `x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 4y - 12 = 0` can represent a circle.
- `2x^2 + 2y^2 - 8x + 10y - 4 = 0` can represent a circle because the squared coefficients match.
- `x^2 + 4y^2 - 6x + 8y = 0` is not a circle because the squared coefficients are different.
- `x^2 - y^2 + 2x - 6 = 0` is not a circle because the squared terms have opposite signs.

## Worked Example

Identify whether this equation is in the general form of a circle:

```text
x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 4y - 7 = 0
```

Step 1: Check for `x^2` and `y^2`.

Both are present.

Step 2: Check their coefficients.

The coefficient of `x^2` is `1`. The coefficient of `y^2` is also `1`.

Step 3: Identify `D`, `E`, and `F`.

Compare with `x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0`.

| Symbol | Value |
|---|---:|
| `D` | `-10` |
| `E` | `4` |
| `F` | `-7` |

Conclusion: The equation is in the general form of a circle.

## How General Form Connects to Center-Radius Form

General form often comes from expanding center-radius form.

![A preview showing center-radius form expanding into general form.](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-3/topic-general-form-of-a-circle/images/expanded-to-center-radius-preview.svg)

Example:

```text
(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 16
x^2 - 6x + 9 + y^2 + 4y + 4 = 16
x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 3 = 0
```

So the general form is:

```text
x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 3 = 0
```

> [!TIP] What You Are Identifying Today
>
> You do not need to complete the square yet. For this lesson, focus on recognizing the structure and naming the coefficients.

## Guided Practice

### Problem 1

Is `x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 8y + 5 = 0` a possible circle equation in general form?

**Hint:** Check the squared terms first.

**Answer:** Yes. Both `x^2` and `y^2` are present with equal coefficients of `1`.

### Problem 2

Identify `D`, `E`, and `F` in:

```text
x^2 + y^2 - 12x - 6y + 20 = 0
```

**Answer:** `D = -12`, `E = -6`, and `F = 20`.

### Problem 3

Is `x^2 + 3y^2 - 4x + 6y - 2 = 0` a circle?

**Hint:** Compare the coefficients of `x^2` and `y^2`.

**Answer:** No. The squared coefficients are `1` and `3`, so they are not equal.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!WARNING] Misconception 1
>
> Seeing `x^2` and `y^2` is not enough. Their coefficients must also be equal.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 2
>
> The constant term does not tell you by itself whether the equation is a circle. Start by checking the squared terms.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 3
>
> `2x^2 + 2y^2 + 4x - 8y + 6 = 0` can still be a circle equation. Divide every term by `2` to see the standard general-form pattern.

## Error Analysis

A learner says:

```text
x^2 + 9y^2 - 6x + 12y - 4 = 0 is a circle because it has x^2 and y^2.
```

What is the mistake?

> [!CHECK] Explanation
>
> The equation has both squared terms, but their coefficients are not equal. The coefficient of `x^2` is `1`, while the coefficient of `y^2` is `9`. This equation is not a circle.

## Extension Challenge

An equation is written as:

```text
4x^2 + 4y^2 - 24x + 16y - 12 = 0
```

1. Does it represent a possible circle?
2. Divide by the common coefficient of the squared terms.
3. Identify `D`, `E`, and `F` after simplifying.

> [!CHECK] Extension Solution
>
> Yes, it can represent a circle because the squared coefficients are both `4`. Divide every term by `4` to get `x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 3 = 0`. So `D = -6`, `E = 4`, and `F = -3`.
