## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circle | The set of all points the same distance from one center point |
| Center | The fixed point in the middle of a circle, written as (h, k) |
| Radius | The distance from the center to any point on the circle |
| Center-radius form | The form $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$ |
| Radius point | A point on the circle found by moving exactly one radius from the center |
| Sketch | A graph that shows the correct location, size, and shape even if it is drawn by hand |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> To graph a circle, identify the center (h, k), find the radius r, plot the center, then move r units up, down, left, and right to mark points on the circle.

## Main Concept Explanation

### 1. Read the Form Correctly

The center-radius form of a circle is:

$$ (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 $$

The center is **(h, k)** and the radius is **r**.

For example:

$$ (x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 16 $$

Rewrite the second part mentally as:

$$ (y - (-3))^2 $$

So the center is **(2, -3)** and the radius is **4**, because $r^2 = 16$.

> [!WARNING] Sign Check
>
> The signs inside the parentheses are opposites of the center coordinates. $(x - 2)$ means the x-coordinate of the center is 2. $(y + 3)$ means the y-coordinate of the center is -3.

### 2. Plot the Center

The center is the anchor of the circle. If the center is (2, -3), start at the origin, move 2 units right, then 3 units down.

### 3. Use the Radius in Four Directions

From the center, move the radius length:

- right
- left
- up
- down

These four radius points help you sketch the circle with the correct size.

![Plotting four radius points from a center](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-3/topic-graphing-from-center-radius-form/images/plot-radius-points.svg)

### 4. Sketch the Circle

Draw a smooth curve through the radius points. The circle should be centered on (h, k), not on the origin unless the equation says the center is (0, 0).

## Rule Box / Formula Box

| Step | What to Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Match the equation to $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$ |
| 2 | Identify the center (h, k) |
| 3 | Find the radius by taking the square root of $r^2$ |
| 4 | Plot the center |
| 5 | Move r units up, down, left, and right from the center |
| 6 | Sketch a smooth circle through the four radius points |

## Worked Example

Graph the circle:

$$ (x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 9 $$

**Step 1: Identify the center.**

The x-part is $(x - 1)^2$, so h = 1.

The y-part is $(y + 2)^2$, which is $(y - (-2))^2$, so k = -2.

Center: **(1, -2)**

**Step 2: Find the radius.**

The right side is 9, so:

$$ r^2 = 9 $$

$$ r = 3 $$

**Step 3: Plot radius points.**

From (1, -2):

| Direction | Point |
|---|---|
| Right 3 | (4, -2) |
| Left 3 | (-2, -2) |
| Up 3 | (1, 1) |
| Down 3 | (1, -5) |

**Step 4: Sketch the circle through those points.**

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Common Mistake: Using r Squared as the Radius
>
> In $(x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$, the radius is 5, not 25.

> [!WARNING] Common Mistake: Keeping the Parenthesis Signs
>
> In $(x + 4)^2$, the center's x-coordinate is -4. The sign changes because the form is $(x - h)^2$.

> [!WARNING] Common Mistake: Centering Every Circle at the Origin
>
> Only equations like $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$ have center (0, 0). Most circles in this lesson move away from the origin.

## Error Analysis

A student graphs $(x + 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 16$ with center (2, -1) and radius 16.

What went wrong?

<details>
<summary>Reveal mistake explanation</summary>

Two mistakes happened. First, the signs of the center were reversed incorrectly: $(x + 2)^2$ gives x = -2, and $(y - 1)^2$ gives y = 1. Second, the radius is not 16. Since $r^2 = 16$, the radius is 4.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal corrected solution</summary>

The correct center is (-2, 1), and the radius is 4. Radius points are (2, 1), (-6, 1), (-2, 5), and (-2, -3).
</details>

## Final Summary

Graphing a circle from center-radius form is a coordinate process. Read the center from $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, take the square root to find the radius, plot the center, mark four radius points, and sketch a smooth circle through them. The graph is accurate when the center and every radius point match the equation.
