## What You Should Already Know

Before studying circle equations, make sure these ideas feel familiar:

- A coordinate point is written as \((x, y)\).
- The origin is \((0, 0)\).
- Squaring a number means multiplying it by itself.
- A circle is the set of all points the same distance from one fixed point.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circle | The set of all points the same distance from a fixed point |
| Center | The fixed point in the middle of the circle |
| Radius | The distance from the center to any point on the circle |
| Center-radius form | \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\) |
| Point on the circle | A point whose distance from the center is exactly the radius |
| Squared radius | The value \(r^2\) on the right side of the equation |

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> A circle equation is a distance statement. It says, "Every point \((x, y)\) on this circle is \(r\) units from the center \((h, k)\)."

## Visual Introduction

The center is the anchor point. The radius is the fixed distance from that anchor to the circle.

![Circle with center and radius marked](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-3/topic-circle-equation-basics/images/circle-center-radius.svg)

If a point is on the circle, its distance from the center matches the radius. If the distance is shorter, the point is inside. If the distance is longer, the point is outside.

## Main Concept Explanation

### 1. Center-Radius Form

The standard equation for a circle is:

$$
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2
$$

where:

- \((h, k)\) is the center
- \(r\) is the radius
- \(r^2\) is the squared radius

### 2. Reading the Center

In \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\), the center is \((h, k)\).

That means the signs inside the parentheses are connected to subtraction:

| Equation part | Center coordinate |
|---|---|
| \((x - 3)^2\) | \(h = 3\) |
| \((y + 2)^2\) | \(k = -2\), because \(y + 2 = y - (-2)\) |

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> The center uses the opposite-looking sign inside each parenthesis. \((x - 3)^2\) gives \(x\)-coordinate 3, while \((x + 3)^2\) gives \(x\)-coordinate -3.

### 3. Reading the Radius

The right side is \(r^2\), not always \(r\).

For example:

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 25
$$

The squared radius is 25, so the radius is \(r = 5\).

## Worked Example

### Example: Identify the Center and Radius

**Problem:** Identify the center and radius of

$$
(x - 4)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 36
$$

**Solution:**

Compare the equation with \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\).

- \((x - 4)^2\) means \(h = 4\).
- \((y + 3)^2\) means \(k = -3\).
- \(36 = r^2\), so \(r = 6\).

**Answer:** center \((4, -3)\), radius \(6\)

### Guided Problem 2

Find the center and radius of:

$$
(x + 2)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 49
$$

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Rewrite \(x + 2\) as \(x - (-2)\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

The center is not \((2, 4)\). Watch the sign inside the x-parenthesis.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\((x + 2)^2 = (x - (-2))^2\), so \(h = -2\). The y-coordinate is \(4\). Since \(r^2 = 49\), \(r = 7\).

Answer: center \((-2, 4)\), radius \(7\).
</details>

### Guided Problem 3

Does \((3, 4)\) lie on the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)?

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Substitute \(x = 3\) and \(y = 4\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

Check whether \(3^2 + 4^2\) equals 25.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

\(3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25\). The equation is true, so \((3, 4)\) lies on the circle.
</details>

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Misconception 1
>
> The right side of the equation is \(r^2\), not always \(r\). If the equation ends in 36, the radius is 6.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 2
>
> The signs inside the parentheses must be interpreted carefully. \((y + 4)^2\) means the center's y-coordinate is \(-4\).

> [!WARNING] Misconception 3
>
> A point is on a circle only when substitution makes the equation exactly true.

## Error Analysis

A student says:

> For \((x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 16\), the center is \((3, 2)\) and the radius is 16.

Find both mistakes.

<details>
<summary>Reveal mistake explanation</summary>

The x-coordinate should be \(-3\), because \(x + 3 = x - (-3)\). The radius is not 16; \(r^2 = 16\), so \(r = 4\).

Correct answer: center \((-3, 2)\), radius \(4\).
</details>

3. How can substitution tell whether a point is on the circle?

<details>
<summary>Reveal sample responses</summary>

## Final Summary

The center-radius form of a circle is \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\). The center is \((h, k)\), and the radius is the positive value \(r\). A point lies on the circle when substituting its coordinates makes the equation true.
