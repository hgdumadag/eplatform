## What You Should Already Know

Before converting forms, check these ideas:

- Center-radius form looks like $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$.
- The center is $(h, k)$, even though the signs inside the parentheses look opposite.
- Squaring a binomial means multiplying the binomial by itself.
- General form collects all terms on one side: $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$.

### Pre-check / Readiness Quiz

Try these before reading the worked example.

1. What is the center of $(x - 4)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 25$?
2. Expand $(x - 3)^2$.
3. Expand $(y + 2)^2$.
4. If an equation has $= 16$ on the right, what must happen to 16 when writing general form?

<details>
<summary>Reveal pre-check answers</summary>

1. The center is $(4, -1)$.
2. $(x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9$.
3. $(y + 2)^2 = y^2 + 4y + 4$.

If these felt difficult, review binomial expansion before continuing.
</details>

## Main Concept Explanation

### The Two Forms Describe the Same Circle

Center-radius form is useful for seeing the center and radius:

$$ (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 $$

General form is useful for comparing equations and solving algebraically:

$$ x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 $$

To convert from center-radius form to general form, expand both squared binomials, combine constants, and move every term to one side.

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Rule
>
> General form must equal 0. Do not stop at an equation with a number still on the right side.

## Rule Box / Formula Box

Use these binomial patterns:

| Pattern | Expansion |
|---|---|
| $(x - a)^2$ | $x^2 - 2ax + a^2$ |
| $(x + a)^2$ | $x^2 + 2ax + a^2$ |
| $(y - b)^2$ | $y^2 - 2by + b^2$ |
| $(y + b)^2$ | $y^2 + 2by + b^2$ |

Conversion steps:

1. Expand the $x$ binomial.
2. Expand the $y$ binomial.
3. Combine constant terms on the left.
4. Subtract $r^2$ from both sides.
5. Write the equation in the order $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$.

## Worked Example

**Problem:** Convert $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 16$ to general form.

**Step 1: Expand each squared binomial.**

$$ (x - 2)^2 = x^2 - 4x + 4 $$

$$ (y + 3)^2 = y^2 + 6y + 9 $$

**Step 2: Substitute the expansions.**

$$ x^2 - 4x + 4 + y^2 + 6y + 9 = 16 $$

**Step 3: Combine constants.**

$$ x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y + 13 = 16 $$

**Step 4: Move 16 to the left.**

$$ x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y + 13 - 16 = 0 $$

**Step 5: Simplify.**

$$ x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 3 = 0 $$

**Answer:** $x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y - 3 = 0$

> [!CHECK] Quick Check
>
> The center $(2, -3)$ creates the terms $-4x$ and $+6y$. The signs make sense because $(x - 2)^2$ gives a negative $x$ term, and $(y + 3)^2$ gives a positive $y$ term.

## Guided Practice

### Guided Problem 1

Convert $(x - 5)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 9$ to general form.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Expand $(x - 5)^2$ and $(y - 1)^2$ separately.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

You should get $x^2 - 10x + 25 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = 9$ before moving terms.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$$x^2 - 10x + 25 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = 9$$

$$x^2 + y^2 - 10x - 2y + 26 = 9$$

$$x^2 + y^2 - 10x - 2y + 17 = 0$$
</details>

### Guided Problem 2

Convert $(x + 4)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 36$ to general form.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The middle terms will be positive because both binomials use plus signs.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

After expanding, subtract 36 from the constant total.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$$x^2 + 8x + 16 + y^2 + 4y + 4 = 36$$

$$x^2 + y^2 + 8x + 4y + 20 = 36$$

$$x^2 + y^2 + 8x + 4y - 16 = 0$$
</details>

### Guided Problem 3

Convert $(x + 1)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 49$ to general form.

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

$(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

$(y - 6)^2 = y^2 - 12y + 36$.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$$x^2 + 2x + 1 + y^2 - 12y + 36 = 49$$

$$x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 12y + 37 = 49$$

$$x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 12y - 12 = 0$$
</details>

## Independent Practice

Convert each equation to general form.

1. $(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25$
2. $(x + 2)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 16$
3. $(x - 6)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 10$
4. $x^2 + (y - 8)^2 = 64$
5. $(x + 7)^2 + y^2 = 49$
6. $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 9)^2 = 100$

<details>
<summary>Reveal answer key with explanations</summary>

1. $x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y = 0$. The constants $9 + 16$ cancel with 25.
2. $x^2 + y^2 + 4x - 10y + 13 = 0$. The constants $4 + 25 - 16$ give 13.
3. $x^2 + y^2 - 12x + 2y + 27 = 0$. The constants $36 + 1 - 10$ give 27.
4. $x^2 + y^2 - 16y = 0$. The constants $64$ and $64$ cancel.
5. $x^2 + y^2 + 14x = 0$. The constants $49$ and $49$ cancel.
6. $x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 18y - 15 = 0$. The constants $4 + 81 - 100$ give $-15$.
</details>

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Common Traps
>
> - Do not change $(x - 3)^2$ into $x^2 - 9$. The middle term $-6x$ is missing.
> - Do not forget to move $r^2$ to the left side.
> - Do not use the center signs directly as the general-form signs. Expand first.
> - Do not combine $x^2$ and $y^2$ into one variable term.

## Error Analysis

A student converted $(x - 4)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 25$ like this:

$$x^2 - 16 + y^2 + 1 = 25$$

$$x^2 + y^2 - 40 = 0$$

What went wrong?

<details>
<summary>Reveal mistake and correction</summary>

The student squared only the first and last terms and forgot the middle terms.

Correct expansion:

$$x^2 - 8x + 16 + y^2 + 2y + 1 = 25$$

$$x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 2y + 17 = 25$$

$$x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 2y - 8 = 0$$
</details>

## Self-Explanation Prompts

Use these prompts to check whether you understand the conversion, not just the arithmetic.

1. Why does general form need all terms on one side?
2. How can the sign inside a binomial help predict the sign of the middle term?
3. Why is $(x - 5)^2$ not equal to $x^2 - 25$?
4. How can you check whether a final constant term is reasonable?

<details>
<summary>Reveal sample responses</summary>

1. General form is defined with 0 on one side, so moving all terms makes the equation match the standard pattern.
2. A minus sign usually gives a negative middle term, and a plus sign gives a positive middle term.
3. Squaring a binomial means multiplying the whole binomial by itself, which creates a middle term.
4. Add the two squared center numbers, then subtract $r^2$.
</details>

## Extension Challenge

Find a center-radius equation whose general form is:

$$x^2 + y^2 - 14x + 6y + 33 = 0$$

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work backward by completing the square: group the $x$ terms and $y$ terms.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal solution</summary>

$$x^2 - 14x + y^2 + 6y + 33 = 0$$

$$x^2 - 14x + 49 + y^2 + 6y + 9 = -33 + 49 + 9$$

$$(x - 7)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25$$

The center is $(7, -3)$ and the radius is 5.
</details>
