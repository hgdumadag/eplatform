## What You Should Already Know

Before starting this task, make sure these ideas are familiar:

- A point is written as \((x, y)\).
- The center-radius form of a circle is \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\).
- The center is \((h, k)\).
- The radius is \(r\), not always the number on the right side.
- A graph and an equation must describe the same circle.

### Pre-check / Readiness Quiz

Try these before reading the rest of the lesson.

1. What is the center of \((x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 16\)?
2. What is the radius of \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)?
3. Write the equation of a circle with center \((-1, 4)\) and radius \(3\).
4. Does \((5, 0)\) lie on \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)?

<details>
<summary>Reveal pre-check answers</summary>

1. The center is \((3, -2)\).
2. The radius is \(5\).
3. \((x + 1)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 9\).
4. Yes. \(5^2 + 0^2 = 25\).

If you missed more than one item, review center-radius form before completing the design task.
</details>

## Task Overview

Your goal is to design a simple coordinate-plane logo using circles.

Your final work should include:

- at least three circles,
- a coordinate graph or clear sketch,
- the center and radius of each circle,
- the center-radius equation of each circle,
- a short explanation of how the circles create the logo,
- at least one check showing a point on a circle satisfies its equation.

> [!TARGET] Design Requirement
>
> A complete logo is not only a drawing. It is a drawing with mathematical evidence.

## Main Concept Explanation

### Circle equations turn design choices into math

When you choose a circle in your logo, you are choosing:

1. where the circle is centered,
2. how large the circle is,
3. how it sits relative to the other circles.

If a circle has center \((h, k)\) and radius \(r\), its equation is:

$$
(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2
$$

For example, a circle centered at \((2, -1)\) with radius \(4\) has equation:

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 16
$$

### A strong design is organized

Before drawing, make a small plan:

| Design choice | Math question |
|---|---|
| Main outer circle | What center and radius frame the logo? |
| Smaller inner circle | Where should the detail be placed? |
| Repeated circles | Do their centers line up or form symmetry? |
| Touching circles | Are the distances between centers reasonable? |
| Labeling | Can someone match every equation to a graph feature? |

## Worked Example

### Example: A Three-Circle Target Logo

Suppose a logo uses these circles:

| Circle | Center | Radius | Equation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Outer ring | \((0, 0)\) | \(6\) | \(x^2 + y^2 = 36\) |
| Middle ring | \((0, 0)\) | \(3\) | \(x^2 + y^2 = 9\) |
| Accent circle | \((3, 0)\) | \(1\) | \((x - 3)^2 + y^2 = 1\) |

![Worked example showing center radius and equation for one circle in a logo](/content/grade-10/math/quarter-3/topic-quarter-circle-graphing-task/images/circle-logo-worked-example.svg)

To check the accent circle, use the point \((4, 0)\). Substitute into \((x - 3)^2 + y^2 = 1\):

$$
(4 - 3)^2 + 0^2 = 1^2 + 0 = 1
$$

The point satisfies the equation, so \((4, 0)\) is on the accent circle.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!WARNING] Misconception 1: Using the radius instead of radius squared
>
> If the radius is \(5\), the equation must end with \(25\), not \(5\).

> [!WARNING] Misconception 2: Copying the signs from the center
>
> Center \((-4, 2)\) gives \((x + 4)^2 + (y - 2)^2\), not \((x - 4)^2 + (y + 2)^2\).

> [!WARNING] Misconception 3: Drawing first and guessing later
>
> You can sketch creatively, but the final graph must match exact centers and radii.

## Error Analysis

A student designs a circle centered at \((-2, 3)\) with radius \(4\) and writes:

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 4
$$

Find the mistakes.

<details>
<summary>Reveal mistake explanation</summary>

There are two errors. The signs are wrong for the center, and the right side should be the squared radius. Center \((-2, 3)\) gives \((x + 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2\). Radius \(4\) gives \(r^2 = 16\).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal corrected equation</summary>

\[
(x + 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 16
\]
</details>

## Self-Explanation Prompts

Answer these in your notebook or study document.

1. How does the center of a circle affect the equation?
2. Why does the equation use \(r^2\) instead of \(r\)?
3. How can you prove that a point is on one circle in your logo?
4. What makes your logo mathematically clear, not just visually interesting?

<details>
<summary>Reveal sample responses</summary>

1. The center determines the values inside the parentheses.
2. The formula is based on squared distances, so the right side is the squared radius.
3. Substitute the point into the circle equation and check whether both sides are equal.
4. The centers, radii, equations, and graph labels all match.
</details>

## Extension Challenge

Design a logo with one large circle and two smaller circles that are symmetric across the y-axis.

Requirements:

- The large circle must be centered at the origin.
- The two smaller circles must have equal radii.
- The smaller circle centers must be opposite x-values, such as \((-3, 2)\) and \((3, 2)\).
- Write all three equations.

<details>
<summary>Reveal one possible solution</summary>

Large circle: \(x^2 + y^2 = 36\).  
Left small circle: \((x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 4\).  
Right small circle: \((x - 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 4\).
</details>
