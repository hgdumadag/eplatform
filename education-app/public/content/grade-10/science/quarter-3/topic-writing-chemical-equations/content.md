## What You Should Already Know

Before starting, review these ideas:

- Elements have symbols, such as `H` for hydrogen, `O` for oxygen, and `Mg` for magnesium.
- Compounds have formulas, such as `H2O` for water and `CO2` for carbon dioxide.
- A chemical reaction changes starting substances into new substances.
- Atoms are rearranged during a reaction.
- Subscripts in formulas are part of the identity of a substance.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Quick clue |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical equation | A shorthand way to represent a chemical reaction | Shows what reacts and what forms |
| Reactant | A starting substance in a reaction | Left of the arrow |
| Product | A new substance formed in a reaction | Right of the arrow |
| Word equation | An equation written with substance names | `hydrogen + oxygen -> water` |
| Formula equation | An equation written with symbols and formulas | `H2 + O2 -> H2O` |
| Arrow | Symbol that means yields, produces, or forms | Points from reactants to products |
| Coefficient | A number in front of a formula | `2` in `2H2O` |
| Subscript | A small number inside a formula | `2` in `H2O` |

## Main Concept Explanation

### 1. Word Equations Use Names

A **word equation** names the reactants and products.

```text
hydrogen + oxygen -> water
```

Read it as: hydrogen reacts with oxygen to produce water.

The plus sign separates substances on the same side. It means "and" or "reacts with," not ordinary addition.

### 2. Formula Equations Use Symbols and Formulas

A **formula equation** replaces names with chemical symbols and formulas.

```text
H2 + O2 -> H2O
```

This formula equation tells the same story as the word equation, but in the shorthand language of chemistry.

This lesson focuses on translating names into formulas. Balancing equations comes next.

### 3. The Arrow Separates Starting Substances From New Substances

Use the arrow to organize your thinking:

| Left of arrow | Arrow | Right of arrow |
|---|---|---|
| Reactants | `->` means yields, produces, or forms | Products |
| Starting substances | Direction of reaction | New substances |
| `methane + oxygen` | `->` | `carbon dioxide + water` |

### 4. Do Not Change Subscripts to Balance

Subscripts are part of the formula. Changing a subscript changes the substance.

For example:

- `H2O` is water.
- `H2O2` is hydrogen peroxide.

Those are different substances. If an equation needs balancing, change **coefficients**, not subscripts.

> [!IMPORTANT] Key Idea Box
>
> To write a formula equation, keep the reaction order from the word equation: **reactants on the left, products on the right**. Replace each substance name with its correct formula. A coefficient changes how many particles are shown, but a subscript is part of the substance formula.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Simple Word Equation to Formula Equation

Translate:

```text
carbon + oxygen -> carbon dioxide
```

Step 1: Identify reactants and product.

| Reactants | Product |
|---|---|
| carbon, oxygen | carbon dioxide |

Step 2: Replace names with formulas.

| Name | Formula |
|---|---|
| carbon | `C` |
| oxygen gas | `O2` |
| carbon dioxide | `CO2` |

Answer:

```text
C + O2 -> CO2
```

### Example 2: Combustion of Methane

Translate:

```text
methane + oxygen -> carbon dioxide + water
```

Step 1: Keep the same order.

| Word form | Formula form |
|---|---|
| methane | `CH4` |
| oxygen | `O2` |
| carbon dioxide | `CO2` |
| water | `H2O` |

Step 2: Write reactants on the left and products on the right.

```text
CH4 + O2 -> CO2 + H2O
```

This equation is not balanced yet. That is okay for this lesson. The formula translation is correct.

### Example 3: Understanding `2H2O`

Interpret:

```text
2H2O
```

- The coefficient `2` means there are two water molecules.
- The subscript `2` after `H` means each water molecule has two hydrogen atoms.
- The missing subscript after `O` means one oxygen atom per water molecule.

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Common Trap: The Arrow Is Not an Equal Sign
>
> The arrow shows the direction of the reaction. It means reactants produce products. It does not mean the left side and right side are identical substances.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap: Do Not Invent Formulas
>
> Do not write water as `HO` just because the name has hydrogen and oxygen. Use the correct known formula: `H2O`.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap: Coefficients and Subscripts Are Different
>
> `2H2O` and `H2O2` do not mean the same thing. `2H2O` means two water molecules. `H2O2` is hydrogen peroxide.

## Error Analysis

A student translates:

```text
hydrogen + oxygen -> water
```

as:

```text
H + O -> HO
```

What went wrong?

| Error | Why it matters | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Used `H` instead of `H2` | Hydrogen gas usually exists as diatomic molecules | Use `H2` |
| Used `O` instead of `O2` | Oxygen gas usually exists as diatomic molecules | Use `O2` |
| Wrote `HO` for water | Water has the fixed formula `H2O` | Use `H2O` |

Correct formula equation:

```text
H2 + O2 -> H2O
```

Remember: this is translated correctly, but it is not yet balanced.

## Final Summary

A chemical equation is a compact way to describe a reaction. A word equation uses names, while a formula equation uses symbols and formulas. Reactants go on the left of the arrow, products go on the right, and the arrow means yields or produces. When writing formula equations, use correct formulas and remember that coefficients count particles while subscripts belong to the substance formula.
