## What You Should Already Know

Before starting, review these ideas:

- The substances before the arrow are **reactants**.
- The substances after the arrow are **products**.
- The arrow means "yields" or "produces."
- A coefficient, such as the 2 in `2H2O`, tells how many particles or formula units are involved.
- A chemical reaction rearranges atoms into new substances.

## Quick Readiness Check

Try these before reading the explanation.

2. In `2H2O -> 2H2 + O2`, how many different product substances are shown?
3. What does the arrow in a chemical equation mean?

Answers: reactants are on the left; there are two product substances, `H2` and `O2`; the arrow means yields or produces.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Quick clue |
|---|---|---|
| Reactant | A starting substance in a reaction | Before the arrow |
| Product | A substance formed by a reaction | After the arrow |
| Combination reaction | Two or more substances join to form one product | Many become one |
| Synthesis reaction | Another name for a combination reaction | Build together |
| Decomposition reaction | One substance breaks into two or more simpler substances | One becomes many |
| Reaction pattern | The general form of a reaction | Example: `A + B -> AB` |

## Main Concept Explanation

### Combination Reactions

A **combination reaction** happens when two or more reactants join to form a single product. It is also called a **synthesis reaction**.

General pattern:

```text
A + B -> AB
```

The most important clue is the number of substances:

- left side: two or more reactant substances
- right side: one product substance

Example:

```text
2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO
```

Magnesium and oxygen combine to form magnesium oxide. Since there are two reactant substances and one product substance, this is a combination reaction.

### Decomposition Reactions

A **decomposition reaction** happens when one reactant breaks down into two or more products.

General pattern:

```text
AB -> A + B
```

The key clue is again the number of substances:

- left side: one reactant substance
- right side: two or more product substances

Example:

```text
2H2O -> 2H2 + O2
```

Water breaks down into hydrogen gas and oxygen gas. Since one reactant forms more than one product, this is a decomposition reaction.

> [!IMPORTANT] Key Idea Box
>
> To classify these reactions, first count the different substances on each side of the arrow. **Many reactants to one product** usually means combination. **One reactant to many products** usually means decomposition.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Classify a Reaction That Builds

Classify:

```text
2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O
```

Step 1: Count the different reactant substances: `H2` and `O2`, so there are two.

Step 2: Count the different product substances: `H2O`, so there is one.

Step 3: Match the pattern: `A + B -> AB`.

Answer: This is a **combination reaction**.

### Example 2: Classify a Reaction That Breaks

Classify:

```text
2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2
```

Step 1: Count the different reactant substances: `H2O2`, so there is one.

Step 2: Count the different product substances: `H2O` and `O2`, so there are two.

Step 3: Match the pattern: `AB -> A + B`.

Answer: This is a **decomposition reaction**.

### Example 3: Watch the Formula, Not Just the Coefficients

Classify:

```text
4Fe + 3O2 -> 2Fe2O3
```

There are two different reactant substances, `Fe` and `O2`, and one product substance, `Fe2O3`. The coefficients help balance the equation, but they do not change the pattern.

Answer: This is a **combination reaction**.

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Misconception 1: "The bigger formula is always the product."
>
> Not always. In decomposition, a larger compound may be the reactant because it breaks apart.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 2: "Coefficients decide the reaction type."
>
> Coefficients balance atoms. To classify combination and decomposition reactions, count the different substances on each side, not just the coefficient numbers.

> [!WARNING] Misconception 3: "Any reaction with oxygen is combination."
>
> Oxygen appears in many reaction types. Use the pattern, not just the presence of oxygen.

## Error Analysis

A student says:

> `2H2O -> 2H2 + O2` is a combination reaction because hydrogen and oxygen can combine to make water.

What went wrong?

The student is thinking about the reverse reaction instead of the equation shown. The equation starts with water as the only reactant and produces hydrogen and oxygen. The pattern is `AB -> A + B`, so the equation shown is a **decomposition reaction**.

Now analyze this one:

> `2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO` is decomposition because oxygen separates into atoms.

Correction: The equation has two reactant substances and one product substance. Magnesium and oxygen form magnesium oxide, so it is a **combination reaction**.

## Final Summary

Combination and decomposition reactions are opposite patterns. In a **combination reaction**, two or more reactants join to form one product: `A + B -> AB`. In a **decomposition reaction**, one reactant breaks apart to form two or more products: `AB -> A + B`. When classifying, count the different substances on each side of the arrow and match the pattern.
