## What You Should Already Know

Before starting, review these ideas:

- **Reactants** are written on the left side of the arrow.
- **Products** are written on the right side of the arrow.
- A chemical formula, such as `H2O`, tells which atoms are in a substance.
- A **coefficient**, such as the 2 in `2H2`, tells how many particles or formula units are present.
- A chemical reaction rearranges atoms into new substances.

## Quick Readiness Check

2. How many hydrogen atoms are in `2H2`?
3. If 12 g of carbon reacts with 32 g of oxygen, what total mass entered the reaction?

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Quick clue |
|---|---|---|
| Law of Conservation of Mass | Matter is not created or destroyed in a chemical reaction | Mass in = mass out |
| Reactant | A starting substance in a reaction | Before the arrow |
| Product | A substance formed by a reaction | After the arrow |
| Balanced equation | An equation with the same number of each atom on both sides | Equal atom counts |
| Coefficient | A number placed before a formula to show how many particles or formula units | Changes the amount |
| Subscript | A small number inside a formula, such as the 2 in `H2O` | Part of the formula |

## Main Concept Explanation

The **Law of Conservation of Mass** states that matter is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. In a closed system, the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.

This law explains why equations must be balanced. A chemical equation is balanced when each element has the same number of atoms on both sides of the arrow.

### Mass In Equals Mass Out

When carbon burns in oxygen, carbon dioxide forms:

```text
C + O2 -> CO2
```

If 12 g of carbon reacts completely with 32 g of oxygen, the product must have a mass of 44 g.

| Reactant or product | Mass |
|---|---:|
| Carbon used | 12 g |
| Oxygen used | 32 g |
| Total reactant mass | 44 g |
| Carbon dioxide formed | 44 g |

The atoms are arranged differently in carbon dioxide, but the total mass stays the same.

### Atom Counts Must Match

Balancing is a way of showing conservation of atoms on paper. You may change **coefficients**, but you must not change **subscripts** in correct formulas.

| Equation | H atoms left | O atoms left | H atoms right | O atoms right | Balanced? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `H2 + O2 -> H2O` | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | No |
| `2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O` | 4 | 2 | 4 | 2 | Yes |

Changing `H2O` to `H2O2` would not be balancing water. It would make a different substance, hydrogen peroxide.

> [!IMPORTANT] Key Idea Box
>
> A balanced equation is a conservation statement: every atom present before the reaction is still present after the reaction. The atoms may be in new substances, but none are created, destroyed, or lost.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Use Mass Conservation

Problem: A reaction uses 10 g of reactant A and 8 g of reactant B. If the reaction happens in a closed container, what is the total mass of products?

Step 1: Add the masses of the reactants.

```text
10 g + 8 g = 18 g
```

Step 2: Apply conservation of mass.

Answer: The products have a total mass of **18 g**.

### Example 2: Check an Equation by Counting Atoms

Check whether this equation is balanced:

```text
N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3
```

| Element | Reactant side | Product side |
|---|---:|---:|
| N | 2 | 2 |
| H | 6 | 6 |

Answer: The equation is **balanced** because nitrogen and hydrogen have equal atom counts on both sides.

### Example 3: Spot the Problem

Check this equation:

```text
Mg + O2 -> MgO
```

| Element | Reactant side | Product side |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mg | 1 | 1 |
| O | 2 | 1 |

Answer: This is **not balanced** because oxygen has 2 atoms on the reactant side but only 1 atom on the product side. A balanced version is:

```text
2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO
```

## Misconception Alerts

> [!WARNING] Common Trap: "The product is new, so new atoms were made."
>
> New substances form because old atoms are rearranged. New atoms are not created.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap: "Balancing means changing formulas."
>
> Balance by changing coefficients only. Changing a subscript changes the compound.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap: "If mass seems missing, the law failed."
>
> In an open container, a gas can escape. In a closed system, the total mass is conserved.

## Error Analysis

A student balances hydrogen and oxygen like this:

```text
H2 + O2 -> H2O2
```

What went wrong?

| Student move | Why it is a problem | Better move |
|---|---|---|
| Changed `H2O` to `H2O2` | This changes the product from water to hydrogen peroxide | Keep `H2O` and adjust coefficients |
| Focused only on making oxygen equal | Hydrogen and oxygen must both match | Count every element |
| Ignored substance identity | Formulas name real substances | Use correct formulas first, then balance |

A correct balanced equation for forming water is:

```text
2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O
```

## Final Summary

Chemical reactions make new substances by rearranging atoms. The Law of Conservation of Mass says that matter is not created or destroyed, so the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products in a closed system. A balanced equation shows this same idea with atom counts: each element must appear the same number of times on both sides of the arrow.
