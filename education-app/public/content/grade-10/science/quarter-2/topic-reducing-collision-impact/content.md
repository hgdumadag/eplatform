## Key Vocabulary

- **seatbelt** — a restraint that holds a passenger during sudden stops
- **airbag** — a cushion that inflates to extend stopping time
- **crumple zone** — part of a vehicle designed to deform during a crash
- **impact force** — force experienced during collision
- **safety design** — engineering features that reduce injury risk

## Main Concept Explanation

- Safety devices reduce peak force by increasing the time over which momentum changes.
- Seatbelts hold passengers securely and stretch slightly.
- Airbags spread force over a larger area and longer time.
- Crumple zones deform to absorb energy and lengthen the crash.

These ideas come from the Grade 10 Quarter 2 MATATAG physics module. Focus on relationships: what changes, what stays the same, and what evidence would support your explanation.

## Misconception Alerts

- A safer crash is not one with no force; it is one where forces are managed and peak forces are reduced.
- Do not treat formulas or diagrams as decorations. They are tools for explaining relationships.
- If two situations look similar, compare the variables carefully before deciding they have the same outcome.
