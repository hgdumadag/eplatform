# Evidence for Evolution

> [!GOAL] Learning Goal
>
> By the end of this lesson, you should be able to use multiple lines of evidence to explain why scientists conclude that organisms have changed over time and share common ancestry.

## Start Here

A single clue can be misleading. A fossil sequence, island species pattern, limb-bone comparison, and DNA similarity pointing the same way is much stronger.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fossil** | preserved remains or traces of past life | bones, shells, footprints |
| **Biogeography** | study of where organisms live and why | island species related to mainland species |
| **Homologous structure** | similar structure inherited from a common ancestor | human arm and bat wing bones |
| **Common ancestor** | ancestral population shared by later groups | ancestor of whales and land mammals |
| **Molecular evidence** | DNA or protein similarities | similar genes in related organisms |

## Core Explanation

- Fossils show that different organisms lived at different times and that forms can change through rock layers.
- Biogeography shows patterns of distribution that make sense when populations migrate, become isolated, and change.
- Comparative anatomy shows that different functions can be built from similar underlying structures.
- Molecular evidence gives another independent test: closely related organisms usually share more DNA similarities.

> [!IMPORTANT] Core Idea
>
> Fossils show that different organisms lived at different times and that forms can change through rock layers.

## Worked Example: Mammal forelimbs

1. A human arm, bat wing, and whale flipper perform different jobs.
2. Their bone patterns are arranged in similar ways.
3. The best explanation is inheritance from a common ancestor followed by modification.
4. This is evidence for common ancestry, not proof from one example alone.

**What this shows:** Evolutionary evidence is strongest when independent lines support the same explanation.

## Compare The Cases

| Case | What happens | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Fossils | time sequence | older and younger forms in layers |
| Biogeography | place pattern | island species resembling nearby mainland relatives |
| Anatomy | structure pattern | similar limb bones |
| Molecules | DNA pattern | shared genes and protein sequences |

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> One fossil does not prove every detail. Scientists compare many observations and test which explanation fits best.
