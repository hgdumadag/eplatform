# Cylinder Anatomy

> [!GOAL] Lesson Goal
>
> Identify the radius, diameter, height, base, and curved surface of a cylinder so you can label cylinder models correctly.

## Quick Look

Think about a can of sardines, a water tank, a tumbler, or a roll of paper towels. Each one is shaped like a cylinder or close to one.

Ask yourself: **What parts of the object would you measure before finding how much it can hold?**

| Cylinder part | What it means | Everyday example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base | One of the flat circular faces | The top or bottom of a can |
| Radius | Distance from the center of a circular base to its edge | Halfway from the center of a lid to the rim |
| Diameter | Distance across a circular base through the center | The full width of a round lid |
| Height | Perpendicular distance between the two bases | How tall the can or tank stands |
| Curved surface | The rounded side surface connecting the bases | The label wrapped around a can |

## What You Should Already Know

Try these before reading on.

1. What shape is the top of most cans?
2. Which is longer: the radius or the diameter of the same circle?
3. If the diameter of a lid is 12 cm, what is its radius?

Answers: The top is usually a circle. The diameter is longer. The radius is 6 cm because it is half the diameter.

## Core Idea 1: A Cylinder Has Two Circular Bases

A cylinder has **two congruent circular bases**. Congruent means the bases have the same size and shape.

On a can, the top circle and bottom circle are the bases. On a vertical water tank, the circular top and circular bottom are also the bases.

> [!EXAMPLE] Worked Example
>
> A soup can stands upright on a table.
>
> The circle touching the table is one base. The circle on top is the other base. The metal side between them is not a base; it is the curved surface.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> A cylinder does not have only one base. It has two circular bases, even if one base is hidden because the object is standing on it.

## Core Idea 2: Radius and Diameter Are Measured on the Base

The **radius** and **diameter** are parts of the circular base.

- The radius starts at the center of the circle and ends at the edge.
- The diameter goes from one edge of the circle to the opposite edge through the center.
- The diameter is twice the radius.
- The radius is half the diameter.

$$d = 2r$$

$$r = \frac{d}{2}$$

> [!EXAMPLE] Worked Example
>
> A round tank cover has a diameter of 80 cm.
>
> The radius is half of 80 cm:
>
> $$r = \frac{80}{2} = 40$$
>
> The radius is 40 cm.

> [!TIP] Measurement Tip
>
> If a problem gives the full width across the circular top, it is probably giving the diameter. If it gives the distance from the center to the edge, it is giving the radius.

## Core Idea 3: Height Connects the Bases

The **height** of a cylinder is the perpendicular distance between the two circular bases.

For a can standing upright, the height is how tall the can is from bottom base to top base. For a horizontal pipe, the height of the cylinder still means the distance between its two circular ends, not how high it is above the ground.

> [!EXAMPLE] Worked Example
>
> A cylindrical water tank is 2 m tall. Its circular top has a radius of 0.5 m.
>
> The height is 2 m because it measures the distance from the bottom base to the top base. The radius is 0.5 m because it measures from the center of the circular top to its edge.

## Core Idea 4: The Curved Surface Wraps Around the Cylinder

The **curved surface** is the rounded side of the cylinder. It connects the two circular bases.

Imagine peeling the paper label off a can. That label covers the curved surface, not the circular bases.

| Object | Bases | Curved surface |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soda can | Top and bottom circles | Printed side label |
| Round water tank | Circular top and bottom | Rounded wall around the tank |
| Cardboard tube | Two circular ends | Long rounded side |

## How to Label a Cylinder

When labeling a cylinder model:

1. Circle or name the two circular bases.
2. Mark the center of one base.
3. Draw the radius from the center to the edge.
4. Draw the diameter across the circle through the center.
5. Draw the height between the two bases.
6. Shade or describe the curved surface around the side.

> [!CHECK] Mini Check
>
> A cylindrical can has a circular top that is 10 cm across and a height of 14 cm.
>
> 1. What is the diameter?
> 2. What is the radius?
> 3. Which part is covered by the paper label?
>
> Answers: The diameter is 10 cm. The radius is 5 cm. The paper label covers the curved surface.

With a partner:

- point to both bases
- estimate or measure the diameter of one base
- find the radius from the diameter
- measure or estimate the height
- describe where the curved surface is

Use complete labels: **radius**, **diameter**, **height**, **base**, and **curved surface**.
