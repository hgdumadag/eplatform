# Deriving Cylinder Volume

> [!GOAL] Learning Goal
>
> Understand why the volume of a cylinder is the area of its circular base multiplied by its height:  
> \(V = \pi r^2 h\)

## Quick Look

Imagine a stack of identical round coins. One coin shows the **circular base**. The height of the stack tells how many equal layers are piled up.

A cylinder works the same way. It is made of many matching circular layers.

| Part of the cylinder | What it means | Formula connection |
|---|---|---|
| Radius, \(r\) | Distance from the center of the circular base to its edge | Used in \(\pi r^2\) |
| Base area | Area of one circular layer | \(A = \pi r^2\) |
| Height, \(h\) | Distance between the two bases | Number of layers stacked upward |
| Volume | Space inside the cylinder | \(V = \text{base area} \times \text{height}\) |

## What You Should Already Know

Try these before reading further.

1. What is the formula for the area of a circle?
2. If a circle has radius \(4\) cm, what expression gives its area?
3. What unit should be used for volume: cm, cm², or cm³?

Answers: \(A = \pi r^2\); \(\pi(4)^2 = 16\pi\) cm²; volume uses cm³.

## Core Idea: Volume Is Stacked Area

For any prism-like solid with matching bases, volume can be thought of as:

\[
V = \text{area of one base} \times \text{height}
\]

A cylinder is not a prism because its base is curved, but it still has the same idea:

- Every horizontal slice is the same circle.
- The circular base has area \(\pi r^2\).
- The height tells how far those equal circles are stacked.

So:

\[
V = \text{base area} \times \text{height}
\]

\[
V = \pi r^2 \times h
\]

\[
V = \pi r^2 h
\]

> [!IMPORTANT] Meaning of the Formula
>
> In \(V = \pi r^2 h\), the part \(\pi r^2\) finds the area of the circular base. Multiplying by \(h\) stacks that base area through the height of the cylinder.

## Worked Example

Find the volume of a cylinder with radius \(3\) cm and height \(8\) cm. Use \(\pi \approx 3.14\).

**Step 1: Find the base area.**

\[
A = \pi r^2
\]

\[
A = 3.14(3)^2
\]

\[
A = 3.14(9) = 28.26
\]

The circular base area is \(28.26\) cm².

**Step 2: Multiply by the height.**

\[
V = \text{base area} \times h
\]

\[
V = 28.26 \times 8 = 226.08
\]

The volume is **226.08 cm³**.

## Why the Unit Is Cubic

The base area is measured in square units, such as cm². When you multiply by height in centimeters, the units become:

\[
\text{cm}^2 \times \text{cm} = \text{cm}^3
\]

That is why cylinder volume is written in cubic units.

> [!WARNING] Common Mistake
>
> Do not multiply by the diameter unless the formula asks for radius. If the diameter is given, divide by 2 first to find the radius.

## Guided Try

A cylinder has radius \(5\) m and height \(6\) m. Use \(\pi \approx 3.14\).

1. Base area: \(3.14(5)^2 = 3.14(25) = 78.5\) m²
2. Volume: \(78.5 \times 6 = 471\) m³

So the volume is **471 m³**.

## Summary

- A cylinder has two congruent circular bases.
- The area of one base is \(A = \pi r^2\).
- The height shows how far the base is stacked.
- The cylinder volume formula is:

\[
V = \pi r^2 h
\]
