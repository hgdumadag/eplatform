# Measurement and Geometry - Lesson 6: Cylinder Word Problems

> [!GOAL]
> Apply cylinder volume to real containers such as cans, tanks, and tubes. Interpret each answer as capacity and check whether it makes sense.

## Warm-Up: What Does the Number Mean?

Look at a water bottle, food can, paint can, or round tank.

Ask yourself:

- What circular part is the base?
- Is the given width a radius or a diameter?
- Is the question asking for volume in cubic units or capacity in milliliters or liters?

Cylinder word problems are not only about calculating. You must also explain what the answer means in the situation.

## Key Ideas

| Situation clue | What to do |
|---|---|
| The problem gives a radius | Use it directly in `V = pi r^2 h` |
| The problem gives a diameter | Divide by 2 first to find the radius |
| Measurements are in centimeters | Volume is in cubic centimeters, or `cm^3` |
| Capacity is needed | Use `1 cm^3 = 1 mL` and `1000 mL = 1 L` |
| A real container is described | Round reasonably and write a sentence answer |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Use the radius in the formula, not the diameter. If a can is 8 cm wide, its radius is 4 cm.

## Formula Review

For a cylinder:

`V = pi r^2 h`

where:

- `r` is the radius of the circular base
- `h` is the height of the cylinder
- `pi` is about `3.14`

The units are cubic units, such as `cm^3`, `m^3`, or `in^3`.

## Capacity Connections

For water, juice, soup, and similar containers, volume often describes capacity.

| Volume | Capacity |
|---|---|
| `1 cm^3` | `1 mL` |
| `1000 cm^3` | `1000 mL` |
| `1000 mL` | `1 L` |
| `1,000,000 cm^3` | `1000 L` or `1 m^3` |

So a cylinder with volume `750 cm^3` can hold about `750 mL`, or `0.75 L`.

> [!TIP]
> A typical soda can is a few hundred milliliters, not thousands of liters. Use everyday estimates to catch unit mistakes.

## Worked Example 1: Soup Can

A soup can has radius `4 cm` and height `10 cm`. About how many milliliters can it hold?

Step 1: Write the formula.

`V = pi r^2 h`

Step 2: Substitute.

`V = 3.14(4^2)(10)`

Step 3: Calculate.

`4^2 = 16`

`V = 3.14(16)(10) = 502.4`

Step 4: Interpret.

The volume is about `502.4 cm^3`, so the can holds about `502.4 mL`.

Sentence answer: The soup can can hold about `502 mL`, which is a little more than half a liter.

## Worked Example 2: Diameter Is Given

A cylindrical water tank has diameter `2 m` and height `3 m`. Find its volume.

Step 1: Convert diameter to radius.

`r = 2 / 2 = 1 m`

Step 2: Use the cylinder formula.

`V = 3.14(1^2)(3) = 9.42`

The tank volume is about `9.42 m^3`.

Reasonableness check: The tank is taller than a person and 2 meters wide, so a volume larger than `1 m^3` is reasonable.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not write `9.42 m` for a volume. Cylinder volume must use cubic units, such as `m^3` or `cm^3`.

## Worked Example 3: Does It Fit?

A cylindrical container has radius `5 cm` and height `12 cm`. A recipe needs `1 L` of liquid. Can the container hold it?

Find the volume:

`V = 3.14(5^2)(12)`

`V = 3.14(25)(12) = 942`

The volume is `942 cm^3`, so the capacity is `942 mL`.

Compare:

`1 L = 1000 mL`

Since `942 mL < 1000 mL`, the container cannot hold the full liter.

Sentence answer: No. It is short by about `58 mL`.

## Problem-Solving Routine

Use this routine for cylinder word problems:

1. Draw or imagine the cylinder.
2. Label the radius and height.
3. If diameter is given, divide by 2.
4. Substitute into `V = pi r^2 h`.
5. Keep cubic units.
6. Convert capacity units if needed.
7. Write a sentence that answers the real question.

## Guided Practice

Use `pi = 3.14`.

1. A can has radius `3 cm` and height `11 cm`. Find its volume in `cm^3`.
2. A jar has diameter `10 cm` and height `8 cm`. Find its capacity in milliliters.
3. A small tank has radius `0.5 m` and height `2 m`. Find its volume in `m^3`.
4. A container holds `628 cm^3`. How many milliliters is that? How many liters is that?
5. A cylindrical cup has volume `848 mL`. Is it closer to `0.8 L` or `8 L`?

Answers:

1. `310.86 cm^3`
2. `628 mL`
3. `1.57 m^3`
4. `628 mL`, or `0.628 L`
5. It is closer to `0.8 L`

- decide whether a container can hold a stated amount
- explain whether an answer is reasonable in context
