# Measurement and Geometry - Lesson 9: Rectangular Pyramid Volume

> [!GOAL]
> Calculate and estimate the volume of rectangular pyramids. Use the correct formula, label answers with cubic units, and explain what the volume means in a real situation.

## Warm-Up: A Box and a Pyramid

Imagine a rectangular gift box and a pyramid-shaped display stand with the same rectangular base and the same height.

Ask yourself:

- Which solid would hold more space inside?
- What dimensions would you need to measure?
- Why might the pyramid be less than the matching rectangular prism?

A pyramid with the same base and height as a prism has one-third of the prism's volume.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rectangular pyramid | A pyramid with a rectangular base |
| Length, `l` | One side length of the rectangular base |
| Width, `w` | The other side length of the rectangular base |
| Height, `h` | The perpendicular distance from the base to the top vertex |
| Base area | The area of the rectangular base, `l x w` |
| Volume | The amount of space inside a solid, measured in cubic units |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The height in the formula is the straight up-and-down height from the base to the vertex. It is not the slanted edge along a triangular face.

## Formula

For a rectangular pyramid:

`V = (1/3)lwh`

This is the same as:

`V = (1/3)(base area)(height)`

where:

- `l` is the length of the rectangular base
- `w` is the width of the rectangular base
- `h` is the perpendicular height

If the dimensions are in centimeters, the volume is in cubic centimeters, or `cm^3`. If the dimensions are in meters, the volume is in cubic meters, or `m^3`.

## Why One-Third?

A rectangular prism has volume:

`V = lwh`

A rectangular pyramid with the same base and height takes up one-third as much space:

`V = (1/3)lwh`

You can think of the pyramid as a shape that narrows from the base to one point. That narrowing is why the factor `(1/3)` appears.

## Worked Example 1: Calculate Exactly

A rectangular pyramid has a base that is `12 cm` long and `6 cm` wide. Its height is `9 cm`. Find the volume.

Step 1: Write the formula.

`V = (1/3)lwh`

Step 2: Substitute the dimensions.

`V = (1/3)(12)(6)(9)`

Step 3: Multiply.

`12 x 6 x 9 = 648`

Step 4: Divide by 3.

`648 / 3 = 216`

The volume is `216 cm^3`.

> [!CHECK]
> The answer uses cubic centimeters because all three dimensions were measured in centimeters.

## Worked Example 2: Estimate First

A model roof is shaped like a rectangular pyramid. Its base is about `19.8 cm` by `10.2 cm`, and its height is about `6.1 cm`. Estimate the volume.

Round the dimensions:

- `19.8 cm` is about `20 cm`
- `10.2 cm` is about `10 cm`
- `6.1 cm` is about `6 cm`

Estimate:

`V = (1/3)(20)(10)(6)`

`20 x 10 x 6 = 1200`

`1200 / 3 = 400`

The estimated volume is about `400 cm^3`.

Estimates help you check whether an exact answer is reasonable.

## Worked Example 3: Context and Units

A tent display has the shape of a rectangular pyramid. The base is `3 m` long and `2 m` wide. The height is `1.5 m`. About how much space is inside?

`V = (1/3)(3)(2)(1.5)`

`3 x 2 x 1.5 = 9`

`9 / 3 = 3`

The volume is `3 m^3`.

Sentence answer: The display encloses about `3 cubic meters` of space.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not label this answer as `3 m` or `3 m^2`. Volume is three-dimensional, so the unit must be cubic: `m^3`.

## Problem-Solving Routine

Use this routine for rectangular pyramid volume problems:

1. Identify the rectangular base.
2. Label the length and width of the base.
3. Find the perpendicular height.
4. Substitute into `V = (1/3)lwh`.
5. Multiply the three dimensions.
6. Divide by 3.
7. Attach the correct cubic unit.
8. Write a sentence if the problem has a real context.

## Estimation Strategy

When the numbers are decimals or measurements from a real object, estimate before solving.

| Dimension | Good estimate |
|---|---|
| `7.9 cm` | `8 cm` |
| `12.1 cm` | `12 cm` |
| `4.8 m` | `5 m` |
| `29.6 mm` | `30 mm` |

Then use:

`estimated volume = (1/3)(rounded length)(rounded width)(rounded height)`

> [!TIP]
> If your exact answer is very far from your estimate, check whether you forgot to divide by 3 or used a slanted edge instead of the height.

## Guided Practice

1. A rectangular pyramid has `l = 9 cm`, `w = 5 cm`, and `h = 6 cm`. Find the volume.
2. A pyramid has base `10 m` by `4 m` and height `3 m`. Find the volume.
3. Estimate the volume of a rectangular pyramid with dimensions `11.8 cm`, `5.2 cm`, and `6.1 cm`.
4. A student says the volume of a pyramid with dimensions `8 cm`, `3 cm`, and `6 cm` is `144 cm^3`. What mistake did the student make?
5. A small display pyramid has volume `40 in^3`. Explain what the unit `in^3` means.

Answers:

1. `90 cm^3`
2. `40 m^3`
3. About `(1/3)(12)(5)(6) = 120 cm^3`
4. The student found `lwh` but forgot to divide by 3. The correct volume is `48 cm^3`.
5. Cubic inches measure three-dimensional space. One cubic inch is the space inside a cube that is 1 inch on each edge.

## Final Checklist

Before taking the practice exam, make sure you can:

- use `V = (1/3)lwh` for rectangular pyramids
- identify length, width, and perpendicular height
- estimate by rounding dimensions first
- divide by 3 after multiplying the dimensions
- write answers with cubic units such as `cm^3`, `m^3`, or `in^3`
- explain the answer in the context of a container, model, roof, or display

> [!PRACTICE]
> Use the practice questions for quick calculation and estimation checks. Use the assessment when you can solve with correct units and explain your answer in context.
