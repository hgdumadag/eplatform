# Polygon Challenge Problems

> [!GOAL] Lesson Goal
>
> Solve polygon challenge problems by combining vocabulary, side count, regularity, convexity, interior angles, exterior angles, and equations.

## Warm-Up: Read the Clues

A floor tile is shaped like a regular octagon. Each corner has the same measure. A second tile is a pentagon with one corner pushed inward.

Ask yourself:

1. Which tile is regular?
2. Which tile might be non-convex?
3. What angle relationships could help prove your answer?

Challenge problems usually hide the important fact inside a sentence. Your first job is to translate words into a diagram and an equation.

## Tools You Will Use

| Clue | What It Usually Means |
|---|---|
| Regular polygon | All sides are equal and all angles are equal |
| Convex polygon | Every interior angle is less than 180 degrees |
| Non-convex polygon | At least one interior angle is greater than 180 degrees |
| Exterior angle adjacent to an interior angle | The two angles form a straight angle, so they add to 180 degrees |
| One exterior angle of a regular polygon | Use \(360 \div n\), where \(n\) is the number of sides |
| Interior angle sum | Use \((n - 2) \times 180\) degrees |

> [!IMPORTANT] Main Strategy
>
> Draw or describe the polygon first. Label the known values. Then write one equation before calculating.

## Challenge Type 1: Unknown Interior or Exterior Angle

An exterior angle and its adjacent interior angle form a straight line.

\[
\text{interior angle} + \text{exterior angle} = 180^\circ
\]

### Worked Example 1

Diagram described in text: A convex pentagon has one side extended at vertex A. The exterior angle at A is labeled 64 degrees. The adjacent interior angle is labeled \(x\).

Equation:

\[
x + 64 = 180
\]

\[
x = 116
\]

The interior angle is **116 degrees**.

> [!CHECK] Reasoning Prompt
>
> Why is the answer not 64 degrees? Because the 64-degree angle is outside the polygon. The interior angle beside it must complete a straight angle.

## Challenge Type 2: Number of Sides from a Regular Polygon

For a regular polygon, all exterior angles are equal and the exterior angles add to 360 degrees.

\[
\text{one exterior angle} = 360 \div n
\]

So:

\[
n = 360 \div \text{one exterior angle}
\]

### Worked Example 2

Diagram described in text: A regular polygon has one exterior angle labeled 45 degrees at each vertex.

\[
n = 360 \div 45 = 8
\]

The polygon has **8 sides**, so it is an **octagon**.

## Challenge Type 3: Interior Angle Sum

The sum of the interior angles of an \(n\)-sided polygon is:

\[
(n - 2) \times 180^\circ
\]

### Worked Example 3

A hexagon has five interior angles measuring 120 degrees, 110 degrees, 130 degrees, 95 degrees, and 145 degrees. Find the sixth angle.

For a hexagon:

\[
(6 - 2) \times 180 = 720
\]

Known angle sum:

\[
120 + 110 + 130 + 95 + 145 = 600
\]

Missing angle:

\[
720 - 600 = 120
\]

The sixth angle is **120 degrees**.

## Challenge Type 4: Classify from Clues

Sometimes the answer is not just a number. You may need to defend a classification.

Example: A polygon has 6 sides, all sides equal, and one interior angle of 210 degrees.

- It is a hexagon because it has 6 sides.
- It is non-convex because 210 degrees is greater than 180 degrees.
- It is not regular because a regular polygon must be convex with all interior angles equal and less than 180 degrees.

> [!WARNING] Common Trap
>
> Equal sides alone do not prove a polygon is regular. A regular polygon needs equal sides and equal angles.

## Guided Challenge

Diagram described in text: A regular decagon has one exterior angle labeled \(x\). Another drawing shows a convex quadrilateral with angles 80 degrees, 105 degrees, 95 degrees, and \(y\).

1. For the decagon, write \(x = 360 \div 10\). So \(x = 36^\circ\).
2. For the quadrilateral, write \(80 + 105 + 95 + y = 360\). So \(y = 80^\circ\).

Notice that the method changes depending on the clue:

- regular polygon exterior angle: divide 360 by the number of sides;
- quadrilateral angle sum: use 360 degrees total.

## Mini Practice

Solve or explain each item.

1. A regular pentagon has equal exterior angles. Find one exterior angle.
2. A polygon has one exterior angle of 30 degrees and is regular. How many sides does it have?
3. A convex quadrilateral has angles 70 degrees, 100 degrees, 85 degrees, and \(x\). Find \(x\).
4. A polygon has one interior angle of 225 degrees. What does this prove about convexity?

Before taking the practice quiz, make sure you can:

- use \((n - 2) \times 180^\circ\) for interior angle sums;
- explain regular, irregular, convex, and non-convex classifications.
