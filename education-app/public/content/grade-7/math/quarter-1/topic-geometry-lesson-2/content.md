# Drawing Polygons

> [!GOAL] Learning Goal
>
> By the end of this lesson, you should be able to draw polygons with 5, 6, 8, or 10 sides using a ruler and protractor, then check whether the side lengths and angle measures match the given conditions.

## What You Should Already Know

Before drawing polygons, review these ideas:

- A **side** is a straight line segment of a polygon.
- A **vertex** is a corner where two sides meet.
- An **angle** is formed by two sides that meet at a vertex.
- A **regular polygon** has all sides equal and all angles equal.
- An **irregular polygon** does not have all sides equal and all angles equal.

## Opening: Polygons Around You

Look at a stop sign, a floor tile, a honeycomb pattern, or a simple badge shape. Each one can be described by its straight sides and corners.

Ask yourself:

- How many sides does the shape have?
- Do the sides look equal?
- Do the angles look equal?
- Could you draw the shape accurately with a ruler and protractor?

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pentagon | A polygon with 5 sides |
| Hexagon | A polygon with 6 sides |
| Octagon | A polygon with 8 sides |
| Decagon | A polygon with 10 sides |
| Regular polygon | A polygon with all sides equal and all angles equal |
| Irregular polygon | A polygon that is not regular |
| Interior angle | An angle inside the polygon at a vertex |
| Tolerance | An allowed small difference caused by hand drawing or measuring |

## Core Idea: Draw, Then Check

A good polygon drawing is not just a sketch. It should match the required number of sides, side lengths, and angle measures as closely as possible.

Use this construction cycle:

1. **Plan** the number of sides and whether the polygon is regular or irregular.
2. **Draw** each side with a ruler.
3. **Turn** at each vertex using a protractor when angle measures are given.
4. **Close** the figure so the last side meets the starting point.
5. **Check** all side lengths and angle measures.

> [!IMPORTANT] Measurement Tolerances
>
> Hand-drawn figures are rarely perfect. Unless your teacher gives a different rule, a side length within **1 mm** and an angle within **2 degrees** is usually accurate enough for classroom construction work.

## Tool Setup

### Ruler

Use the same unit throughout the drawing. If a side is given as 4 cm, do not switch to inches or grid squares halfway through.

### Protractor

To measure or draw an angle:

1. Place the protractor center on the vertex.
2. Align the zero line with one side of the angle.
3. Count from the correct zero scale.
4. Mark the required degree measure.
5. Draw the next side through that mark.

> [!WARNING] Common Protractor Mistake
>
> Many protractors have two number scales. If the angle should be acute but your mark creates an obtuse angle, you probably read the wrong scale.

## Method 1: Drawing an Irregular Polygon From Given Sides

An irregular polygon can have unequal side lengths and unequal angles. The easiest classroom method is to use graph paper and choose clear vertices.

### Worked Example 1: Draw an Irregular Pentagon

**Task:** Draw a 5-sided irregular polygon.

One possible plan:

1. Mark point A on graph paper.
2. Draw side AB 4 cm to the right.
3. From B, draw side BC 3 cm slanting upward.
4. From C, draw side CD 2 cm slanting left.
5. From D, draw side DE 3 cm downward-left.
6. Connect E back to A.
7. Count the sides: AB, BC, CD, DE, EA.

Check:

- The figure has 5 sides.
- The sides are not all equal.
- The angles are not all equal.
- The polygon is irregular.

> [!CHECK] Quick Check
>
> If a drawing has 5 vertices but one side crosses another side, ask your teacher whether the task requires a simple polygon. Most Grade 7 construction tasks expect sides not to cross.

## Method 2: Drawing a Regular Polygon Using Exterior Turns

For a regular polygon, all side lengths are equal and all angle measures are equal. A practical way to construct one is to use equal side lengths and equal **exterior turns**.

The exterior turn for a regular polygon is:

$$
\text{exterior angle} = \frac{360^\circ}{n}
$$

where \(n\) is the number of sides.

| Regular polygon | Number of sides | Exterior turn |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pentagon | 5 | \(72^\circ\) |
| Hexagon | 6 | \(60^\circ\) |
| Octagon | 8 | \(45^\circ\) |
| Decagon | 10 | \(36^\circ\) |

### Worked Example 2: Draw a Regular Hexagon With 3 cm Sides

**Task:** Draw a regular hexagon with side length 3 cm.

Steps:

1. Draw side AB = 3 cm.
2. At B, use the protractor to make a \(60^\circ\) exterior turn from the direction of AB.
3. Draw BC = 3 cm.
4. Repeat the \(60^\circ\) turn and 3 cm side until you have six sides.
5. The sixth side should return to point A.
6. Check each side with a ruler.
7. Check each interior angle. A regular hexagon should have \(120^\circ\) interior angles.

Why the interior angle is \(120^\circ\):

$$
180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ
$$

## Method 3: Drawing From Given Interior Angles

Sometimes the task gives interior angles instead of exterior turns.

When you draw the next side, remember:

$$
\text{exterior turn} = 180^\circ - \text{interior angle}
$$

### Worked Example 3: Draw Part of a Polygon With a Given Angle

**Task:** Draw two connected sides with an interior angle of \(110^\circ\) at B.

Steps:

1. Draw AB = 4 cm.
2. Place the protractor center at B.
3. Align the zero line with BA.
4. Mark \(110^\circ\) inside the polygon.
5. Draw ray BC through the mark.
6. Measure the required length for BC.

Check:

- The angle at B should measure about \(110^\circ\).
- If your measurement is between \(108^\circ\) and \(112^\circ\), it is within the 2-degree tolerance.

## Regular vs Irregular Examples

| Drawing description | Regular or irregular? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A pentagon with all sides 4 cm and all angles \(108^\circ\) | Regular | Equal sides and equal angles |
| A hexagon with sides 3 cm, 3 cm, 4 cm, 3 cm, 5 cm, 4 cm | Irregular | Side lengths are not all equal |
| An octagon with all sides 2 cm but angles not equal | Irregular | Equal sides alone is not enough |
| A decagon with all angles equal but side lengths different | Irregular | Equal angles alone is not enough |

> [!TIP] Regular Polygon Test
>
> To call a polygon regular, check **both** conditions: all sides equal and all angles equal.

## Measurement Checklist

After drawing, make a table like this:

| Item to check | What to record | Acceptable result |
|---|---|---|
| Number of sides | Count the side segments | Matches the task |
| Side lengths | Measure each side | Within 1 mm of the target length |
| Angle measures | Measure each interior angle | Within 2 degrees of the target angle |
| Closure | Last side meets the first vertex | No large gap or overlap |
| Regularity | Compare sides and angles | Matches regular or irregular requirement |

## Pair Task: Draw and Check

Work with a partner if possible.

1. Partner A draws an irregular pentagon or hexagon.
2. Partner B checks the number of sides, side lengths, and angle measures.
3. Partner B writes one improvement suggestion.
4. Switch roles.

Use this sentence frame:

> The polygon is a ______ because it has ______ sides. It is ______ because ______.

## Independent Practice

Complete these in your notebook.

1. Draw an irregular pentagon. Label the vertices A, B, C, D, and E.
2. Draw a regular hexagon with each side 3 cm.
3. Draw a regular octagon using exterior turns of \(45^\circ\).
4. Draw any decagon, then measure and record three of its interior angles.
5. A classmate draws a "regular pentagon" with side lengths 4 cm, 4 cm, 4 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm. Explain what needs to be fixed.

<summary>Reveal practice guidance</summary>

1. The pentagon should have exactly 5 straight sides and should close.
2. A regular hexagon should have 6 equal sides and angles close to \(120^\circ\).
3. A regular octagon should have 8 equal sides and exterior turns close to \(45^\circ\).
4. A decagon should have 10 sides.
5. The 5 cm side must be corrected because a regular pentagon needs all sides equal.

## Final Checklist

Before taking the practice quiz, make sure you can:

- Count the sides of a polygon you drew.
- Use a ruler to draw and check side lengths.
- Use a protractor to draw and check angles.
- Draw polygons with 5, 6, 8, or 10 sides.
- Explain why a polygon is regular or irregular.
- Apply a tolerance such as 1 mm for sides and 2 degrees for angles.
