# Convex and Non-Convex Polygons

> [!GOAL] Lesson Goal
>
> Sort polygons by shape behavior. By the end, you should be able to decide whether a polygon is convex or non-convex and justify your answer using interior angles or diagonals.

## Warm-Up: What Do You Notice?

Look around a classroom, notebook, or tiled floor. Some shapes look like they bulge outward. Others may have a dent or inward corner.

Answer mentally:

1. Does the shape have only straight sides?
2. Is it closed?
3. Does any corner point inward?

If a closed shape has only straight sides, it is a polygon. Today we focus on how the polygon behaves.

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Convex polygon | A polygon with no inward dent. Every interior angle is less than 180 degrees. |
| Non-convex polygon | A polygon with at least one inward dent. It has at least one interior angle greater than 180 degrees. |
| Interior angle | An angle inside the polygon at a vertex. |
| Diagonal | A segment that connects two non-adjacent vertices. |

> [!IMPORTANT] Main Test
>
> A polygon is **convex** if every diagonal stays inside the polygon. A polygon is **non-convex** if at least one diagonal goes outside the polygon.

## Core Idea 1: Use Interior Angles

A convex polygon does not cave inward. Because of this, each interior angle is smaller than a straight angle.

- Convex: all interior angles are less than 180 degrees.
- Non-convex: at least one interior angle is greater than 180 degrees.

### Worked Example

A pentagon has one inward corner. At that vertex, the inside angle measures 220 degrees.

Since 220 degrees is greater than 180 degrees, the pentagon is **non-convex**.

> [!TIP] Quick Reason
>
> One angle greater than 180 degrees is enough to prove that a polygon is non-convex.

## Core Idea 2: Use Diagonals

Imagine drawing diagonals between vertices.

For a convex polygon, every diagonal stays inside the shape. For a non-convex polygon, at least one diagonal crosses outside the shape because of the inward dent.

### Worked Example

A hexagon has no inward corners. When diagonals are drawn between non-adjacent vertices, they all stay inside or on the polygon.

This hexagon is **convex**.

## Compare the Two Types

| Feature | Convex Polygon | Non-Convex Polygon |
|---|---|---|
| Shape behavior | Bulges outward | Has an inward dent |
| Interior angles | All are less than 180 degrees | At least one is greater than 180 degrees |
| Diagonals | Stay inside | At least one goes outside |
| Other name sometimes used | Convex | Concave |

## Guided Task

For each description, classify the polygon.

1. A quadrilateral with angles 80 degrees, 100 degrees, 90 degrees, and 90 degrees.
2. A pentagon with one interior angle of 210 degrees.
3. A polygon where one diagonal from vertex A to vertex D passes outside the shape.

### Check Your Thinking

1. Convex, because all angles are less than 180 degrees.
2. Non-convex, because 210 degrees is greater than 180 degrees.
3. Non-convex, because at least one diagonal goes outside.

## Common Mistakes

> [!WARNING] Watch For These
>
> Do not classify by number of sides alone. A pentagon, hexagon, or decagon can be convex or non-convex depending on its shape.

> [!WARNING] Another Trap
>
> A polygon can look unusual and still be convex. The key question is whether it has an inward dent or an interior angle greater than 180 degrees.
