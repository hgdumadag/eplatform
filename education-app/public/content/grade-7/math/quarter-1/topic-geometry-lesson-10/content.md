# Geometry - Lesson 10: Quarter Polygon Portfolio

> [!GOAL]
> Create a polygon reference sheet with accurate drawings, labels, classifications, angle relationships, and solved examples. Be ready to defend why each classification and measurement is correct.

## Warm-Up: What Belongs in a Polygon Portfolio?

Imagine a classmate missed the whole polygon unit. Your portfolio should help that classmate answer three questions:

- What kind of polygon is this?
- How do I know?
- What angle or side measurement can I calculate from the information given?

Your portfolio is not only an art page. It is a math reference sheet. Every drawing needs labels, and every solved example needs reasoning.

## Portfolio Target

By the end of this lesson, your portfolio should include:

| Portfolio part | What to include | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary panel | polygon, side, vertex, diagonal, regular, irregular, convex, non-convex | Gives precise language |
| Classification examples | at least 5-, 6-, 8-, and 10-sided polygons | Reviews names and side counts |
| Measurement examples | side lengths, angle measures, or angle equations | Shows how measurements support claims |
| Angle relationships | interior, exterior, adjacent interior, complementary, supplementary, vertical, adjacent | Connects the unit ideas |
| Solved examples | at least two calculations with steps | Proves you can use the ideas, not just list them |
| Reflection | one short defense of a classification or measurement | Shows mathematical reasoning |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Keep your portfolio focused on math evidence: diagrams, labels, calculations, definitions, and explanations.

## Quick Review: Polygon Facts

| Term | Meaning | Example or test |
|---|---|---|
| Polygon | A closed plane figure made only of straight line segments | A pentagon is a polygon; a circle is not |
| Side | One straight segment of a polygon | A hexagon has 6 sides |
| Vertex | A corner where two sides meet | A pentagon has 5 vertices |
| Diagonal | A segment connecting non-adjacent vertices | A square has 2 diagonals |
| Regular polygon | All sides equal and all angles equal | A regular octagon |
| Irregular polygon | Not all sides or not all angles are equal | A rectangle that is not a square |
| Convex polygon | All interior angles are less than 180 degrees | A regular decagon |
| Non-convex polygon | At least one interior angle is greater than 180 degrees | A dart-shaped quadrilateral |

## Angle Facts to Use

| Idea | Formula or relationship | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Interior angle sum | `(n - 2) x 180` | An octagon: `(8 - 2) x 180 = 1080 degrees` |
| Regular interior angle | `interior sum / n` | Regular octagon: `1080 / 8 = 135 degrees` |
| Exterior angle next to interior angle | `interior + exterior = 180` | If interior is 135 degrees, exterior is 45 degrees |
| Exterior angle sum for convex polygon | `360 degrees` | Regular decagon exterior angle: `360 / 10 = 36 degrees` |
| Adjacent angles on a line | Add to 180 degrees | `x + 118 = 180` |
| Vertical angles | Opposite angles are equal | If one is 72 degrees, the vertical angle is 72 degrees |

> [!WARNING]
> A regular polygon must pass two tests: equal sides and equal angles. Equal sides alone are not enough.

## Worked Portfolio Entry

Use this model when you create your own entries.

### Entry: Regular Octagon

**Drawing plan:** Draw an 8-sided polygon. Mark all sides with the same tick mark. Label the vertices `A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H`.

**Classification:**

- Number of sides: 8, so it is an octagon.
- Regularity: regular, because all sides and all angles are equal.
- Convexity: convex, because every interior angle is less than 180 degrees.

**Solved measurement example:**

Find each interior angle of a regular octagon.

1. Use the interior angle sum formula: `(n - 2) x 180`.
2. Substitute `n = 8`: `(8 - 2) x 180 = 6 x 180 = 1080`.
3. A regular octagon has 8 equal interior angles.
4. Divide: `1080 / 8 = 135`.

Each interior angle is 135 degrees.

**Exterior angle check:**

The adjacent exterior angle is supplementary to the interior angle.

`135 + x = 180`

`x = 45`

Each exterior angle is 45 degrees. This also matches `360 / 8 = 45`.

**Defense statement:**

This figure is a regular convex octagon because it has 8 sides, its side and angle markings show equality, and each interior angle is 135 degrees, which is less than 180 degrees.

## Build Your Portfolio

Create one neat reference sheet or multi-page portfolio. Use graph paper or digital geometry tools if available.

### Required Entries

Include at least six entries:

1. A pentagon or hexagon with vocabulary labels.
2. A regular polygon with 5, 6, 8, or 10 sides.
3. An irregular polygon with 5, 6, 8, or 10 sides.
4. A convex polygon and a non-convex polygon.
5. A triangle or quadrilateral created from angle conditions.
6. A challenge example that finds an unknown side count or angle measure from clues.

### For Each Entry

Write:

- polygon name
- number of sides and vertices
- regular or irregular
- convex or non-convex
- important labels on the drawing
- one sentence defending the classification

For at least two entries, add a solved measurement example with an equation.

## Guided Practice

Complete these before taking the practice exam.

1. Draw a hexagon. Label one side, one vertex, and one diagonal.
2. Draw or describe a regular pentagon. Explain how you know it is regular.
3. Draw a non-convex polygon. Circle the interior angle that is greater than 180 degrees.
4. Find the interior angle sum of a decagon.
5. A regular hexagon has exterior angles of 60 degrees. Explain how this connects to the full 360-degree turn around the polygon.

Answers and sample responses:

1. Labels may vary, but a diagonal must connect two non-adjacent vertices.
2. A regular pentagon has 5 equal sides and 5 equal angles.
3. The circled reflex interior angle shows why the polygon is non-convex.
4. `(10 - 2) x 180 = 1440 degrees`.
5. A hexagon has 6 equal exterior angles, and `6 x 60 = 360`.
