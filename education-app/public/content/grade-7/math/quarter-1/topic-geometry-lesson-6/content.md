# Geometry - Lesson 6: Angle Pair Relationships

> [!GOAL]
> Describe complementary, supplementary, vertical, and adjacent angles. Use those relationships to find missing angle measures.

## Warm-Up

Look at a pair of scissors, a street crossing, or two sticks crossing on a desk.

Ask yourself:

- Which angles touch each other?
- Which angles are opposite each other?
- Which angles seem to make a straight line?

Angle pairs are useful because they let you find a missing measure without measuring every angle again.

## Key Vocabulary

| Angle pair | What it means | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Complementary angles | Two angles whose measures add to 90 degrees | `a + b = 90` |
| Supplementary angles | Two angles whose measures add to 180 degrees | `a + b = 180` |
| Adjacent angles | Two angles that share a vertex and a side, with no overlap | They are next to each other |
| Vertical angles | Opposite angles formed by two intersecting lines | They are equal |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Adjacent angles must share a vertex and a side. Vertical angles are opposite each other, so they are not adjacent.

## Complementary Angles

Complementary angles make a right angle.

Example: One angle measures 35 degrees. Its complement is unknown.

Set up the equation:

`35 + x = 90`

Solve:

`x = 90 - 35 = 55`

So the missing angle is 55 degrees.

> [!CHECK]
> If one angle is 68 degrees, what is its complement?
>
> `90 - 68 = 22`, so the complement is 22 degrees.

## Supplementary Angles

Supplementary angles make a straight angle.

Example: Two adjacent angles form a straight line. One angle is 124 degrees.

Set up the equation:

`124 + x = 180`

Solve:

`x = 180 - 124 = 56`

The missing angle is 56 degrees.

> [!WARNING]
> A common mistake is using 90 degrees whenever two angles touch. Use 180 degrees when the angles form a straight line.

## Adjacent Angles

Adjacent angles are side by side. They share:

- one vertex
- one side
- no overlapping interior space

Adjacent angles may be complementary, supplementary, or neither. The word adjacent only tells you about position.

Example: Two adjacent angles measure 40 degrees and 50 degrees. They add to 90 degrees, so they are both adjacent and complementary.

## Vertical Angles

Vertical angles are formed when two lines intersect. Opposite angles are vertical angles, and vertical angles are congruent.

Example: Two lines intersect. One angle is 73 degrees. The angle opposite it is `x`.

Because vertical angles are equal:

`x = 73`

The missing angle is 73 degrees.

## Worked Example

Two lines intersect. One angle is 118 degrees.

Find:

- the vertical angle
- one adjacent angle

Solution:

1. The vertical angle is equal to 118 degrees.
2. An adjacent angle forms a straight line with 118 degrees.
3. Write `118 + x = 180`.
4. Solve `x = 62`.

So the vertical angle is 118 degrees, and each adjacent angle is 62 degrees.

## Guided Practice

Solve each missing angle.

1. An angle and its complement form 90 degrees. One angle is 27 degrees. The other angle is ___.
2. An angle and its supplement form 180 degrees. One angle is 143 degrees. The other angle is ___.
3. Two vertical angles are shown. One is 81 degrees. The opposite angle is ___.
4. Two adjacent angles form a straight line. One is 49 degrees. The other is ___.

Answers: 63 degrees, 37 degrees, 81 degrees, 131 degrees.

## Final Checklist

Before taking the practice exam, make sure you can:

- tell whether two angles add to 90 degrees or 180 degrees
- identify adjacent angles from their shared side and vertex
- use equality for vertical angles
- write a simple equation for a missing angle

> Use the practice questions for quick skill checks. Use the assessment when you can explain why each relationship works, not just calculate the missing measure.
