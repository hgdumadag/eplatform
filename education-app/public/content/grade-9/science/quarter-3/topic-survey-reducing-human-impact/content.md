## Main Concept Explanation

A survey gathers information about a local environmental problem so people can understand patterns, identify causes, and choose practical actions.

The important study move is to connect the vocabulary to a clear relationship. Ask yourself: **What parts are involved? What changes or stays the same? What evidence would show that the idea is correct?**

> [!IMPORTANT] Key Idea Box
>
> A survey gathers information about a local environmental problem so people can understand patterns, identify causes, and choose practical actions. For this Grade 9 Biology lesson, a strong answer uses correct vocabulary, a labeled diagram, and a relevant example.

## Worked Examples

| Example | Biological Explanation |
|---|---|

### How to reason through an example

1. Identify the biological level: molecule, cell, organism, population, community, or ecosystem.
2. Identify the key parts or causes.
3. Use a diagram or table to show the relationship.
4. Explain the result using evidence from the situation.

## Misconception Alerts

- A survey is not just asking random questions; it needs a focus and a recording plan.
- A good survey should lead to evidence-based action, not just a list of complaints.
