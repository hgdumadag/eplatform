# The Electromagnetic Spectrum

> [!GOAL]
> Build a clear Grade 9 understanding of **electromagnetic spectrum** using the MATATAG Quarter 1 Physics ideas: forces and motion, electricity, and electromagnetic waves.

## Estimated Time and Difficulty

- **Estimated time:** 50–60 minutes
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Materials:** notebook, pencil, and safe low-voltage circuit materials only when available. Never use household outlets for experiments.

## What You Should Already Know

- Balanced forces do not change an object's motion, while unbalanced forces can.
- Speed, direction, and motion can be described using words, numbers, arrows, and diagrams.
- Simple circuits need a source, conductors, and a complete path.
- Waves transfer energy from one place to another.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **spectrum** | ordered range of related waves |
| **radio wave** | long-wavelength low-frequency EM wave |
| **microwave** | EM wave used in cooking, radar, and communication |
| **ultraviolet** | EM wave beyond violet with enough energy to damage skin |
| **gamma ray** | very high-frequency high-energy EM wave |

## Main Concept Explanation

The electromagnetic spectrum arranges EM waves from longest wavelength and lowest frequency to shortest wavelength and highest frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays. Visible light is only a small part of the whole spectrum.

### Learning Objectives

- List the major parts of the electromagnetic spectrum.
- Compare wavelength, frequency, and energy across the spectrum.
- Locate visible light within the spectrum.
- Infer which waves have higher or lower energy.

> [!NOTE]
> **Key idea:** In Physics, do not only memorize words. Connect each word to a diagram, a cause, and an observable effect.

## Misconception Alerts

- **Common mistake:** The colors we see are not the whole electromagnetic spectrum. They are just a narrow visible band.
- Do not confuse a description with an explanation. A description says what happens; an explanation says why it happens.
- Check whether forces act on the same object or on different objects before deciding if they cancel.

## Error Analysis

A learner says: “The biggest arrow or most energetic wave is always the correct answer.” This is incomplete. A good Physics explanation must match the **specific situation**. Ask: What object or wave is being studied? What quantity changed? What evidence supports the answer?
