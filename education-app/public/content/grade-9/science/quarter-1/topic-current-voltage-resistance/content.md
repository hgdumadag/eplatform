# Current, Voltage, and Resistance

> [!GOAL]
> Build a clear Grade 9 understanding of **current, voltage, and resistance** using the MATATAG Quarter 1 Physics ideas: forces and motion, electricity, and electromagnetic waves.

## Estimated Time and Difficulty

- **Estimated time:** 50–60 minutes
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Materials:** notebook, pencil, and safe low-voltage circuit materials only when available. Never use household outlets for experiments.

## What You Should Already Know

- Balanced forces do not change an object's motion, while unbalanced forces can.
- Speed, direction, and motion can be described using words, numbers, arrows, and diagrams.
- Simple circuits need a source, conductors, and a complete path.
- Waves transfer energy from one place to another.

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **current** | rate of charge flow measured in amperes |
| **voltage** | electric potential difference or push measured in volts |
| **resistance** | opposition to current measured in ohms |
| **load** | device that uses electrical energy |
| **Ohm's Law** | relationship V = IR among voltage, current, and resistance |

## Main Concept Explanation

Current is the amount of charge flowing each second. Voltage is the electrical push provided by a battery or source. Resistance is how strongly a material or device opposes current. For the same resistance, higher voltage causes higher current. For the same voltage, higher resistance causes lower current.

### Learning Objectives

- Define current, voltage, and resistance.
- Describe how voltage pushes current.
- Describe how resistance opposes current.
- Use the idea of Ohm's Law qualitatively.

> [!NOTE]
> **Key idea:** In Physics, do not only memorize words. Connect each word to a diagram, a cause, and an observable effect.

## Misconception Alerts

- **Common mistake:** Voltage is not the same as current. Voltage is the push; current is the flow that results when a circuit path exists.
- Do not confuse a description with an explanation. A description says what happens; an explanation says why it happens.
- Check whether forces act on the same object or on different objects before deciding if they cancel.

## Error Analysis

A learner says: “The biggest arrow or most energetic wave is always the correct answer.” This is incomplete. A good Physics explanation must match the **specific situation**. Ask: What object or wave is being studied? What quantity changed? What evidence supports the answer?
