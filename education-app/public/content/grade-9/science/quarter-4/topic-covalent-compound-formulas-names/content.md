## Main Concept Explanation

### Idea 1: A covalent formula tells how many atoms of each element are in one molecule

A covalent formula tells how many atoms of each element are in one molecule.

### Idea 2: A subscript is the small number written after an element symbol

A subscript is the small number written after an element symbol. It counts atoms of that element only.

### Idea 3: H₂O means two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom

H₂O means two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

### Idea 4: CO₂ means one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms

CO₂ means one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms.

### Idea 5: NH₃ means one nitrogen atom and three hydrogen atoms

NH₃ means one nitrogen atom and three hydrogen atoms.

### Idea 6: Many simple covalent compounds have common names, such as water and ammonia

Many simple covalent compounds have common names, such as water and ammonia.

## Misconception Alerts

- **Trap:** A missing subscript means 1, not 0.
- **Trap:** Subscripts are atom counts in covalent molecules, not charges.
- **Trap:** Do not change subscripts when simply reading a formula.

## Error Analysis

> [!WARNING]
> A learner says CO₂ has two carbon atoms because the 2 is near the formula. The correction: the subscript 2 follows O, so it counts oxygen atoms only.
