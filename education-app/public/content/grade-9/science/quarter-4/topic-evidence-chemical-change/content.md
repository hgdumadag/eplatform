## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| chemical change | A change that forms one or more new substances. |

## Main Concept Explanation

### Idea 1: A chemical change is not just a change in shape, size, or state

A chemical change is not just a change in shape, size, or state. It produces a new substance with properties different from the starting materials.

### Idea 2: Common evidence includes gas formation, color or odor change, energy change, and precipitate formation

Common evidence includes gas formation, color or odor change, energy change, and precipitate formation. The strongest conclusion comes from multiple observations and a controlled investigation.

### Idea 3: In the limewater test, carbon dioxide reacts with clear limewater to form solid calcium carbonate

In the limewater test, carbon dioxide reacts with clear limewater to form solid calcium carbonate. The milky appearance is evidence that a new substance formed.

### Idea 4: A valid investigation changes only the factor being tested

A valid investigation changes only the factor being tested. A reliable investigation repeats trials or uses repeated observations so the pattern is not a one-time accident.

## Key Idea Box

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Use observable evidence and fair-test reasoning to decide whether a chemical change has produced a new substance. In chemistry, strong answers connect **observable evidence** to **particles, electrons, bonds, or structure**.

## Worked Examples

### Limewater test

Carbon dioxide is passed through clear limewater. The liquid turns cloudy because solid calcium carbonate forms.

### Precipitate test

Two clear solutions are mixed. A visible solid appears. This is stronger evidence of chemical change than simply stirring or pouring.

## Misconception Alerts

- **Trap:** Bubbles do not always prove chemical change; boiling water also bubbles.
- **Trap:** A color change can be evidence, but it should be interpreted with the whole situation.
- **Trap:** A fair test needs controlled variables, not just one exciting observation.

## Error Analysis

> [!WARNING]
> A learner says, “It became cloudy because the liquid was stirred.” The mistake is ignoring the new solid. If cloudiness appears only when specific chemicals are mixed and can be repeated, it supports precipitate formation.
