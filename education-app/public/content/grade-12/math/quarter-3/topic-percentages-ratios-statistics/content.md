# Percentages, Ratios, and Statistics

## Introduction

Percentages, ratios, and statistical measures appear extensively in UPCAT mathematics. These concepts are not merely abstract; they connect deeply to real-world scenarios involving sales, budget allocation, data interpretation, and probability. Mastering percentage calculations—including the nuanced skill of compound percentages—is critical for UPCAT success. Additionally, the ability to extract meaning from statistical data through measures like mean, median, and mode, and to interpret visual representations like pie charts and line graphs, forms a crucial skill set. This topic integrates numerical reasoning with practical application, bridging pure mathematics and interpretive analysis.

## Percent Conversions

A percent is a ratio expressed as a fraction of 100. The word "percent" literally means "per hundred."

**Definition**: $n\% = \frac{n}{100}$

**Converting between forms**:
- Percent to decimal: Divide by 100. Example: $35\% = 35 \div 100 = 0.35$
- Decimal to percent: Multiply by 100. Example: $0.72 = 0.72 \times 100 = 72\%$
- Percent to fraction: Write as a fraction with denominator 100, then simplify. Example: $40\% = \frac{40}{100} = \frac{2}{5}$
- Fraction to percent: Convert to a decimal, then multiply by 100. Example: $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375 = 37.5\%$

## Three Types of Percentage Problems

**Type 1: Finding a percentage of a number**

Problem form: What is n% of x?

Formula: $n\% \text{ of } x = \frac{n}{100} \times x$

Example: What is 25% of 80?
- Solution: $0.25 \times 80 = 20$

**Type 2: Finding what percent one number is of another**

Problem form: What percent is a of b?

Formula: $\text{Percent} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100$

Example: What percent is 15 of 60?
- Solution: $\frac{15}{60} \times 100 = 0.25 \times 100 = 25\%$

**Type 3: Finding the whole given a part and percent**

Problem form: 40% of what number is 28?

Formula: $\text{Whole} = \frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Percent/100}}$

Example: 30% of what number is 45?
- Solution: $\text{Whole} = \frac{45}{0.30} = 150$

## Percentage Increase and Decrease

These calculations are essential for real-world applications like pricing, salary changes, and population trends.

**Percentage Increase**:
$$\text{Percent Increase} = \frac{\text{New Value} - \text{Old Value}}{\text{Old Value}} \times 100$$

Example: A price increased from \$80 to \$100. What is the percent increase?
- Solution: $\frac{100 - 80}{80} \times 100 = \frac{20}{80} \times 100 = 25\%$

**Percentage Decrease**:
$$\text{Percent Decrease} = \frac{\text{Old Value} - \text{New Value}}{\text{Old Value}} \times 100$$

Example: A population decreased from 5000 to 4500. What is the percent decrease?
- Solution: $\frac{5000 - 4500}{5000} \times 100 = \frac{500}{5000} \times 100 = 10\%$

## Compound Percentages (UPCAT FAVORITE)

Compound percentages involve applying one percentage to the result of another percentage. This is an UPCAT favorite because it tests understanding beyond basic percent mechanics.

**Formula**: To find a% of b% of n:
$$\text{Result} = \frac{a}{100} \times \frac{b}{100} \times n = \frac{ab}{10000} \times n$$

**Key insight**: The order does not matter. 40% of 30% of x equals 30% of 40% of x.

Example: What is 40% of 30% of 500?
- Solution: $0.4 \times 0.3 \times 500 = 0.12 \times 500 = 60$
- Verification: 30% of 500 = 150; 40% of 150 = 60. ✓

Example: 25% of 80% of a number is 100. Find the number.
- Solution: $0.25 \times 0.8 \times n = 100$
- $0.2n = 100$
- $n = 500$

## Ratios and Proportions

A ratio compares two quantities. A proportion states that two ratios are equal.

**Ratio notation**: $a:b$ reads as "a to b" and equals $\frac{a}{b}$

**Proportion**: If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, then $a:b = c:d$, and we say a, b, c, d are proportional.

**Cross multiplication** (essential technique):
If $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, then $ad = bc$

Example: Solve $\frac{x}{8} = \frac{15}{20}$
- Cross multiply: $20x = 8 \times 15 = 120$
- Solve: $x = 6$

**Dividing quantities in a given ratio**: If 300 is divided in the ratio 2:3, find each part.
- Total parts: 2 + 3 = 5
- Value of one part: $300 \div 5 = 60$
- First quantity: $2 \times 60 = 120$
- Second quantity: $3 \times 60 = 180$
- Verification: $120 + 180 = 300$ ✓

## Statistical Measures

Statistical measures summarize data, revealing patterns and central tendencies.

**Mean (Average)**: The sum of all values divided by the number of values.
$$\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Sum of all values}}{\text{Number of values}}$$

Example: Find the mean of 12, 15, 18, 21, 24
- Solution: $\frac{12 + 15 + 18 + 21 + 24}{5} = \frac{90}{5} = 18$

**Median**: The middle value when data is arranged in ascending or descending order.
- If the number of values is odd, the median is the middle value.
- If the number of values is even, the median is the average of the two middle values.

Example: Find the median of 13, 7, 21, 4, 9, 15, 2, 18
- Sorted: 2, 4, 7, 9, 13, 15, 18, 21
- Median = $\frac{9 + 13}{2} = 11$ (average of two middle values)

**Mode**: The value that appears most frequently in the dataset.
- A dataset can have one mode (unimodal), two modes (bimodal), or no mode if all values appear with equal frequency.

Example: Find the mode of 5, 8, 3, 8, 12, 5, 8, 15
- 8 appears three times; 5 appears twice; others appear once
- Mode = 8

**Range**: The difference between the maximum and minimum values.
$$\text{Range} = \text{Maximum Value} - \text{Minimum Value}$$

Example: Find the range of 3, 7, 12, 5, 18, 9
- Maximum: 18; Minimum: 3
- Range = 18 - 3 = 15

## Basic Probability

Probability measures the likelihood of an event occurring, expressed as a number between 0 and 1 (or 0% and 100%).

**Definition**: For an event with equally likely outcomes:
$$P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of possible outcomes}}$$

Example: What is the probability of rolling a 3 on a fair six-sided die?
- Favorable outcomes: 1 (rolling a 3)
- Total outcomes: 6
- $P(\text{rolling a 3}) = \frac{1}{6}$

**Complementary Events**: The complement of event A, denoted A', includes all outcomes not in A.
$$P(A') = 1 - P(A)$$

Example: The probability of rain is 0.3. What is the probability of no rain?
- $P(\text{no rain}) = 1 - 0.3 = 0.7$

**Mutually Exclusive Events**: Events that cannot occur simultaneously.
$$P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$$

Example: What is the probability of drawing either a heart or a spade from a standard deck?
- $P(\text{heart}) = \frac{13}{52}$, $P(\text{spade}) = \frac{13}{52}$
- $P(\text{heart or spade}) = \frac{13}{52} + \frac{13}{52} = \frac{26}{52} = \frac{1}{2}$

## Graph Interpretation

**Pie Charts**: Circular graphs where each sector represents a portion of the total, with angles proportional to frequencies or percentages.
- The total is always 360°
- A sector representing 25% of data spans 90° (since $0.25 \times 360 = 90$)

**Line Graphs**: Show trends over time, with independent variable on the x-axis and dependent variable on the y-axis.
- Useful for identifying trends, peaks, and valleys
- Multiple lines can compare different data series

**Bar Graphs**: Compare quantities across categories using rectangular bars.
- Horizontal or vertical orientation
- Heights or lengths are proportional to the quantities

## Key Formulas

$$\text{Percentage of a number}: \quad P = \frac{n}{100} \times x$$

$$\text{Percentage change}: \quad \text{Change}\% = \frac{\text{New} - \text{Old}}{\text{Old}} \times 100$$

$$\text{Compound percentage}: \quad a\% \text{ of } b\% = \frac{a \times b}{10000}$$

$$\text{Mean}: \quad \bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n}$$

$$\text{Probability}: \quad P(A) = \frac{\text{Favorable}}{\text{Total}}$$

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Compound Percentage

**Problem**: 40% of 60% of 250 equals what number?

**Solution**:
- Method 1: $0.4 \times 0.6 \times 250 = 0.24 \times 250 = 60$
- Method 2: 60% of 250 = 0.6 × 250 = 150. Then 40% of 150 = 0.4 × 150 = 60

**Answer**: 60

### Example 2: Finding the Whole Given Part and Percent

**Problem**: If 35% of a number is 91, find the number.

**Solution**:
- Let x be the number
- $0.35x = 91$
- $x = \frac{91}{0.35} = \frac{91}{35/100} = \frac{91 \times 100}{35} = \frac{9100}{35} = 260$

**Answer**: 260

### Example 3: Finding Median with Even Number of Values

**Problem**: Find the median of the dataset: 13, 7, 21, 4, 9, 15, 2, 18

**Solution**:
- Sort in ascending order: 2, 4, 7, 9, 13, 15, 18, 21
- Count: 8 values (even)
- Two middle values: 9 and 13 (positions 4 and 5)
- Median = $\frac{9 + 13}{2} = \frac{22}{2} = 11$

**Answer**: 11

### Example 4: Probability with Multiple Conditions

**Problem**: A bag contains 5 red balls, 3 blue balls, and 2 green balls. What is the probability of drawing a ball that is NOT red?

**Solution**:
- Total balls: 5 + 3 + 2 = 10
- Non-red balls: 3 + 2 = 5
- $P(\text{not red}) = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5$

**Answer**: 1/2 or 0.5 or 50%

### Example 5: Percentage Increase from Budget Allocation

**Problem**: In a pie chart, education receives 25% of a ₱200,000 budget. How much is allocated to education?

**Solution**:
- Amount = 25% of ₱200,000
- Amount = $0.25 \times 200,000 = 50,000$

**Answer**: ₱50,000

## UPCAT Tips & Common Mistakes

**Tip 1: Distinguish between "of" and "out of" in percentage language**: "25% of 80" means multiply ($0.25 \times 80 = 20$), while "what percent is 20 out of 80" means divide and multiply by 100 ($\frac{20}{80} \times 100 = 25\%$).

**Tip 2: Compound percentages require multiplication, not addition**: 40% of 30% is NOT 70%; it is $0.4 \times 0.3 = 0.12 = 12\%$ of the original.

**Tip 3: Always identify the whole in percentage problems**: The whole is the quantity you're taking a percentage of. "30% of what?" requires solving for the whole.

**Tip 4: Mean is affected by extreme values; median is not**: A single very large or very small value can dramatically shift the mean, but the median remains stable.

**Tip 5: Cross-multiplication is your friend for proportions**: Convert $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$ to $ad = bc$ to avoid fractions.

**Common Mistake 1**: Reversing numerator and denominator in the percentage formula. Always use $\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100$.

**Common Mistake 2**: Forgetting to convert percentages to decimals or fractions in calculations. Always use 0.25, not 25, when multiplying.

**Common Mistake 3**: Computing percentages on the wrong value. A 10% decrease followed by a 10% increase does not return to the original value due to different bases.

**Common Mistake 4**: Confusing median with mean. Median is the middle value; mean is the average.

## Key Takeaways

1. Percentages express ratios per hundred; master conversions among percent, decimal, and fraction forms.
2. Compound percentages require multiplying decimal equivalents.
3. Three distinct percentage problem types require different approaches; identify the type first.
4. Ratios compare quantities; proportions equate two ratios; cross-multiplication solves proportion equations.
5. Mean, median, and mode each reveal different aspects of data; choose the appropriate measure.
6. Probability ranges from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain); use counting principles to calculate.
7. Visual data interpretation demands careful reading of axes, labels, and scale on graphs.
