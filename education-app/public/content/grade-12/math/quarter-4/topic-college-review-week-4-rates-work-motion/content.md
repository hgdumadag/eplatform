# College Review Week 4: Rates, Work, and Motion

## Weekly Goal

This week turns several confusing word-problem families into one familiar structure: **rate × time = amount**. Once the student sees that motion, work, and similar problems share the same table, setup becomes much more stable.

## Session 1: Motion as a Table

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Warm-Up Recall

1. State the distance formula relating rate, time, and distance.
2. How many hours are in 90 minutes?
3. If a car travels 180 km in 3 hours, what is its average speed?

### Core Formula

$$\text{distance} = \text{rate} \times \text{time}$$

Make a table with three columns: `object`, `rate`, `time`, `distance`.

**Example 1:**  
Two cars start from the same point and travel in opposite directions at 40 kph and 60 kph. After how many hours will they be 300 km apart?

Let $t$ be the time in hours.

- Car 1 distance: $40t$
- Car 2 distance: $60t$

Since they move apart:

$$40t + 60t = 300$$

$$100t = 300$$

$$t = 3$$

## Session 2: Work Problems and Unit Discipline

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Work Formula

$$\text{work done} = \text{rate} \times \text{time}$$

If a person can finish a job in 6 hours, their hourly work rate is:

$$\frac{1}{6} \text{ of the job per hour}$$

**Example 2:**  
Worker A can finish a job in 6 hours, and Worker B can finish it in 3 hours. How long will they take together?

Let $t$ be the time together.

$$\frac{1}{6}t + \frac{1}{3}t = 1$$

$$\frac{1}{6}t + \frac{2}{6}t = 1$$

$$\frac{3}{6}t = 1$$

$$\frac{1}{2}t = 1$$

$$t = 2$$

### Trap: Mixed Units

If one rate is in meters per second and another is in kilometers per hour, convert before solving.

### Example 3: Average Speed Trap

A student travels 60 km at 30 kph and returns 60 km at 60 kph. The average speed is **not** 45 kph by simple averaging.

Total distance:

$$60 + 60 = 120$$

Total time:

$$\frac{60}{30} + \frac{60}{60} = 2 + 1 = 3$$

Average speed:

$$\frac{120}{3} = 40 \text{ kph}$$

## Session 3: Timed Practice and Reflection

**Target time:** 45 minutes

### Mini Drill

1. Two cyclists ride toward each other at 12 kph and 18 kph from towns 90 km apart. When do they meet?
2. A pipe fills a tank in 4 hours. Another pipe fills it in 6 hours. How long together?
3. Convert 1 hour 30 minutes into hours before using a motion equation.

### Final Worked Example

**Example 4:**  
A boat travels 36 km downstream in 3 hours and the same distance upstream in 4.5 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the speed of the current.

Downstream speed:

$$\frac{36}{3} = 12$$

Upstream speed:

$$\frac{36}{4.5} = 8$$

Let $b$ be boat speed in still water and $c$ be current speed.

$$b + c = 12$$
$$b - c = 8$$

Add:

$$2b = 20 \Rightarrow b = 10$$

Then:

$$c = 2$$

### End-of-Week Checklist

- I can make a rate table before solving.
- I can use total distance over total time for average speed.
- I can convert units before building the equation.

## Key Takeaways

- Motion and work are both rate problems.
- Tables reduce confusion and prevent missing terms.
- When you are unsure, write what one hour of work or travel represents.
