# College Review Week 2: Percent Change, Discounts, and Mixtures

## Weekly Goal

This week fixes one of the most common structural weaknesses in college entrance math: failing to notice when the **base quantity changes**. In percent increase, decrease, discount, markup, and mixture problems, the key question is: **Percent of what?**

## Session 1: Percent Change and Changing Bases

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Warm-Up Recall

1. Increase 200 by 15%.
2. Decrease 80 by 25%.
3. A shirt worth 500 pesos is discounted by 20%. What is the new price?
4. A quantity rises from 40 to 50. What is the percent increase?

### Core Idea 1: Percent Change Uses the Original Base

$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{change}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%$$

**Example 1:**  
A score rises from 60 to 75. Find the percent increase.

Change $= 75 - 60 = 15$

$$\frac{15}{60} \times 100\% = 25\%$$

The increase is **25%**.

### Core Idea 2: Discounted Price is Not Original Minus Percent Number

**Example 2:**  
A bag costs 800 pesos and is marked down 15%.

Discount:

$$0.15 \times 800 = 120$$

New price:

$$800 - 120 = 680$$

**Reminder:** A 15% discount does not mean subtract 15. It means subtract 15% of the original price.

## Session 2: Multi-Step Change and Mixtures

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Trap 1: Consecutive Percent Changes Do Not Cancel

If a price increases 20% and then decreases 20%, it does **not** return to the original value because the second percent is based on a different number.

**Example 3:**  
A price rises from 100 to 120, then drops 20%.

Second step:

$$0.20 \times 120 = 24$$

$$120 - 24 = 96$$

The final price is 96, not 100.

### Trap 2: Mixture Problems Need Part + Part = Total

**Example 4:**  
How many liters of 20% acid solution should be mixed with 10 liters of 50% acid solution to make a 32% solution?

Let $x$ be liters of 20% solution.

Pure acid equation:

$$0.20x + 0.50(10) = 0.32(x + 10)$$

$$0.20x + 5 = 0.32x + 3.2$$

$$1.8 = 0.12x$$

$$x = 15$$

So 15 liters of 20% solution are needed.

### Session 2 Reminders

- In two-step percent problems, write the new base after the first step.
- In mixtures, track the amount of pure substance, not just the total liquid.
- If your final concentration is outside the two starting concentrations, something is wrong.

## Session 3: Timed Drill and Reflection

**Target time:** 45 minutes

### Mini Drill

1. A price drops from 1,200 to 960. What percent decrease is this?
2. A salary increases 8% and then 5%. Is that the same as a 13% increase?
3. Mix 5 liters of 40% juice with $x$ liters of 10% juice to make a 25% mixture.

### Final Worked Example

**Example 5:**  
An item is marked up 25% from cost and then sold for 900 pesos. What was the original cost?

Let the cost be $c$.

$$1.25c = 900$$

$$c = 720$$

### End-of-Week Checklist

- I can solve percent increase and decrease from a clear base.
- I can model mixture problems using a pure-part equation.
- I can explain why consecutive percent changes do not simply add or subtract.

## Key Takeaways

- Every percent belongs to a base quantity.
- Multi-step percent problems require new bases at each stage.
- Mixture problems become manageable when written as pure amount = pure amount.
