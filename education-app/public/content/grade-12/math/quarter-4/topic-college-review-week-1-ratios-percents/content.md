# College Review Week 1: Ratios, Fractions, and Percents

## Weekly Goal

This week rebuilds the foundation behind many college entrance math questions: part-whole reasoning, proportions, fraction-decimal-percent conversion, and ratio interpretation. The most important habit is to stop asking "Which operation looks familiar?" and instead ask, **What is the whole? What is the part? What is being compared?**

## Session 1: Recall and Core Structure

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Warm-Up Recall

Before you begin, answer these without a calculator:

1. Convert $\frac{3}{4}$ to a decimal and a percent.
2. Write $0.125$ as a fraction in simplest form.
3. If the ratio of boys to girls is $2:3$, what fraction of the group are girls?
4. What percent of 80 is 12?
5. 15 is 30% of what number?

### Core Idea 1: Ratios Compare, Fractions Part a Whole

A ratio such as $2:3$ compares two quantities. A fraction such as $\frac{2}{5}$ tells you how much of the total is represented by one part.

**Example 1:**  
The ratio of red beads to blue beads is $2:3$. What fraction of the beads are red?

Total parts $= 2 + 3 = 5$  
Red fraction $= \frac{2}{5}$

**Reminder:** When a ratio compares two categories, add the parts first before turning it into a fraction of the whole.

### Core Idea 2: Percent Means "Per 100"

Percent is just another way to write a fraction with denominator 100.

- $25\% = \frac{25}{100} = \frac{1}{4}$
- $0.6 = 60\%$
- $\frac{7}{20} = 35\%$

**Example 2:**  
What percent of 80 is 12?

Set up part over whole:

$$\frac{12}{80} = \frac{x}{100}$$

$$x = 15$$

So 12 is **15%** of 80.

### Session 1 Reminders

- Always label `part`, `whole`, and `comparison`.
- A percent problem is easier when written as a fraction first.
- If the answer is a percent greater than 100, ask whether that makes sense.

## Session 2: Guided Drill and Common Traps

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Trap 1: Switching the Part and the Whole

**Example 3:**  
35 is what percent of 140?

Correct setup:

$$\frac{35}{140} = \frac{x}{100}$$

$$x = 25$$

So the answer is **25%**.

Wrong setup:

$$\frac{140}{35}$$

This reverses the relationship and gives a nonsense result.

### Trap 2: Ratio Language vs Percent Language

**Example 4:**  
In a class, the ratio of students who passed to students who failed is $7:3$. What percent passed?

Total parts $= 10$  
Passed fraction $= \frac{7}{10}$  
Passed percent $= 70\%$

Do **not** say 7% or 70 students unless the problem gives an actual total.

### Mini Drill

Solve these on paper:

1. Write $62.5\%$ as a fraction in simplest form.
2. A map scale is $1:50{,}000$. What real distance is represented by 2 cm on the map?
3. 18 is 45% of what number?
4. A recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio $5:2$. What fraction of the mixture is sugar?

### Session 2 Reminders

- `is` usually points to the part.
- `of` often points to the whole.
- Ratio questions often become percent questions only after you build the whole.

## Session 3: Timed Mini-Set and Error Log

**Target time:** 45 minutes

### Timed Strategy

Do the practice exam for this week after Session 2 or at the start of Session 3. Work the first half at a calm pace, then time yourself more strictly on the second half.

### Error-Log Categories

Tag every mistake as one of these:

- `part-whole`: you reversed the setup
- `conversion`: fraction, decimal, or percent conversion was wrong
- `ratio-translation`: you forgot to add all parts first
- `careless`: arithmetic or copying mistake

### Final Worked Example

**Example 5:**  
A store says 30% of its 250 shirts are blue. How many shirts are blue?

$$0.30 \times 250 = 75$$

So 75 shirts are blue.

### End-of-Week Checklist

- I can convert among fractions, decimals, ratios, and percents.
- I can tell which value is the whole before solving.
- I can explain why my percent answer is reasonable.

## Key Takeaways

- Most weak percent performance starts with weak part-whole thinking.
- Ratios become fractions of the whole only after total parts are found.
- If you cannot name the whole in one sentence, pause and rewrite the problem.

## How to Use the Exams This Week

- Use the **practice exam** as your recall quiz and guided drill.
- Use the **assessment exam** after Session 3 as your weekly checkpoint.
- Review every wrong answer out loud before moving to Week 2.
