# College Review Week 3: Algebra Translation and Modeling

## Weekly Goal

This week targets the habit that most often separates correct and incorrect solutions in word problems: writing the equation **before** manipulating numbers. The student should leave this week able to define variables clearly and turn relationships into equations instead of guessing.

## Session 1: Variable First, Equation Second

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Warm-Up Recall

Translate each statement into an expression or equation:

1. Five more than a number
2. Twice the sum of a number and 3
3. The difference between a number and 8 is 14
4. Three consecutive integers

### Core Idea 1: Define the Variable Clearly

If the problem asks for a number, let:

$$x = \text{the number}$$

If it asks for the smaller number, say so.

**Example 1:**  
The sum of a number and 12 is 31. Find the number.

Let $x$ be the number.

$$x + 12 = 31$$

$$x = 19$$

### Core Idea 2: Use Structure Words Carefully

- `sum` means add
- `difference` means subtract in the order stated
- `product` means multiply
- `is` often means equals

**Example 2:**  
Twice a number decreased by 5 is 17.

Let $x$ be the number.

$$2x - 5 = 17$$

$$2x = 22$$

$$x = 11$$

## Session 2: Common Word-Problem Families

**Target time:** 45-60 minutes

### Consecutive Integers

If the first integer is $x$, then the next two are $x+1$ and $x+2$.

**Example 3:**  
Three consecutive integers have sum 72. Find them.

$$x + (x+1) + (x+2) = 72$$

$$3x + 3 = 72$$

$$3x = 69$$

$$x = 23$$

So the integers are 23, 24, and 25.

### Digit and Number Relationships

**Example 4:**  
The tens digit of a two-digit number is 3 more than the ones digit. Their sum is 11. Find the number.

Let the ones digit be $x$. Then the tens digit is $x+3$.

$$(x+3) + x = 11$$

$$2x = 8$$

$$x = 4$$

Tens digit $= 7$, ones digit $= 4$, so the number is **74**.

### Session 2 Reminders

- Define the unknown in words, not just a symbol.
- Consecutive even integers go $x, x+2, x+4$.
- Always substitute the final answer back into the wording.

## Session 3: Timed Translation and Checking

**Target time:** 45 minutes

### Mini Drill

1. The sum of two numbers is 40 and the larger is 6 more than the smaller.
2. A number increased by one-third of itself is 28.
3. The product of two consecutive integers is 56.

### Final Worked Example

**Example 5:**  
A father is 4 times as old as his son. In 8 years, he will be twice as old as his son. Find their present ages.

Let $s$ be the son's age.  
Then father's age $= 4s$.

In 8 years:

$$4s + 8 = 2(s + 8)$$

$$4s + 8 = 2s + 16$$

$$2s = 8$$

$$s = 4$$

So the son is 4 and the father is 16.

### Error Log

Tag mistakes as:

- `translation`
- `variable choice`
- `equation setup`
- `solve/check`

## Key Takeaways

- Most algebra word problems are easier after one clean variable definition.
- Write the relationship as an equation before solving.
- Check whether your answer fits the story, not just the algebra.

## How to Use the Exams This Week

- Use the **practice exam** as a translation drill with explanations.
- Use the **assessment exam** after Session 3 and force yourself to define the variable first on every question.
- If you miss a problem, rewrite only the setup sentence and equation before solving again.
